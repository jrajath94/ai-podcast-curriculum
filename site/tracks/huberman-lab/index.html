Huberman Lab: AI Track Index

Huberman Lab: AI Track

Guest: Andrew Huberman hosts. Eight episodes with AI-relevant guests, ranked by verified YouTube view counts (October 2026).

Honest gap

Only 8 AI-topic episodes exist on the Huberman Lab channel. The channel’s full catalog of 447 videos was keyword-scanned on October 6, 2026, and 8 genuinely AI-heavy episodes were found. Ranks 9 and 10 do not exist under the AI-topic rule, so they are not fabricated. No episodes with Altman, Hassabis, LeCun, or Ng exist on the channel. This track has 8 chapters, one per available episode.

Two more honest notes. Episode 8 is a clip-cut re-release of episode 1’s conversation, so chapters 1 and 8 share a core. Chapter 8 teaches only what is new or sharpened. Episode 2 is roughly 80 percent geopolitics and 20 percent AI. Chapter 2 says so and does not inflate the AI content.

The episodes

  1. Episode 1: Machines, Creativity & Love | Dr. Lex Fridman: Why it matters. The only episode that defines what a robot is, and the definition reorganizes everything after it. AI thinks. A robot perceives, thinks, and acts.
  2. Episode 2: Navigating Conflict, Finding Purpose & Maintaining Drive | Dr. Lex Fridman: Why it matters. The human operating system behind the AI dream. How hate generalizes, how a costly visible decision coordinates a country, and the company plan that funds robots in every home.
  3. Episode 3: How to Improve at Learning Using Neuroscience & AI | Dr. Terry Sejnowski: Why it matters. The machinery of learning itself. Sleep spindles write the day into cortex, the Ambien experiment proves it, and transformers get the clearest explanation on the track.
  4. Episode 4: How Risk Taking, Innovation & AI Transform Human Experience | Marc Andreessen: Why it matters. The history of why AI looks the way it does. The 1943 fork, the dataset-first argument with numbers, and the precautionary principle’s war on nuclear power as the template for AI policy fights.
  5. Episode 5: Curing All Human Diseases & the Future of Health & Technology | Mark Zuckerberg & Dr. Priscilla Chan: Why it matters. AI aimed at the hardest problem. Machine learning defined as pattern recognition, AlphaFold as the proof, Cell by Gene as the working tool, and the AR glasses ladder.
  6. Episode 6: Neuralink & Technologies to Enhance Human Brains | Dr. Matthew MacDougall: Why it matters. The only episode that opens the skull. Electrode threads thinner than hair, the decode path from motor cortex to cursor, and the near-linear alcohol dose line on cortical thinning.
  7. Episode 7: Using AI to Increase Your Intelligence & Enrich Humanity | Dr. Fei-Fei Li: Why it matters. The ImageNet story from the person who lived it. The 2006 data insight, the 2012 convergence, the triad that still explains every milestone, and the gray-cup limit of AI.
  8. Episode 8: Essentials: Machines, Creativity & Love | Dr. Lex Fridman: Why it matters. The compression test. Episode 1 in 42 minutes, plus the sharpest statements of self-supervised learning, value alignment, the Tesla data engine, and time as the master variable.

The intelligence thread

The “Think it yourself” drills get harder across the track, by design:

  • Chapters 1 and 2: guided drills. The answers sit in the chapter. The work is to reason them out before checking.
  • Chapters 3 and 4: intermediate drills. Real reasoning load, Fermi arithmetic, both-sides arguments.
  • Chapters 5 and 6: harder. Fewer handrails, experimental design, steelmanning.
  • Chapters 7 and 8: near-unaided. First-principles reasoning, proofs, crux-finding.

The track trains the reader to think, in the order the episodes demand it: definitions first, mechanisms next, judgment last.

How to use this track

Read in order. Each chapter assumes the previous chapters’ definitions: robot (ch1), the company-as-lab (ch2), spindles and self-attention (ch3), the 1943 fork and the data triad (ch4), pattern recognition (ch5), decode paths (ch6), ImageNet (ch7). Chapter 8 is the self-test: if you can derive its ideas from the earlier chapters, the track worked.

Fidelity note: reading a chapter equals watching the episode for substantive claims. Sponsor segments are excluded. Transcripts are cleaned captions, not verbatim-certified, so no sentence is presented as a verbatim quote.
