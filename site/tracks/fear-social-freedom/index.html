Fear & Social Freedom: Track Index

2026-10-06 · Instructor: Various · AI Podcast Curriculum

The track

Fear & Social Freedom is track 9 of 15 in the AI Podcast Curriculum. It rebuilds the ten deepest episodes on overcoming fear, anxiety, and social timidity across podcast shows, ranked by verified YouTube view counts (October 2026), as textbook chapters: the full argument of each conversation, numbers worked by hand, figures for every state change, and drills that train the person.

The arc runs from the inside out. Episodes 1 to 2 install the neuroscience: what fear and emotion are in the brain. Episodes 3 to 5 give the protocols: stress tools, arousal reappraisal, and the inner voice. Episodes 6 to 9 move into practice: social anxiety, speaking, confidence, overthinking. Episode 10 zooms out to the environment that manufactures modern fear: the phone-based childhood. Read in order. Each chapter assumes the vocabulary of the ones before.

The intelligence-thread arc

The drills in “Think it yourself” get progressively harder across the track, by design. Early chapters (ep01 to ep03) give guided drills with scaffolding: label the layer, trace the circuit, compute the sigh. The middle chapters (ep04 to ep06) thin the scaffolding: design the loop, price the avoidance, audit your model. The late chapters (ep07 to ep10) demand unaided reasoning: argue both sides from mechanisms, design experiments, write the norm speech, do the 104-year review. The track trains the reader to think from first principles, not just to collect tools. Do every drill with your own brain before touching AI.

Episodes

Each line is why the episode matters: what it gives you that no other episode does.

  1. Ep 1 · Andrew Huberman: the fear circuit itself: amygdala, HPA axis, extinction-before-relearning, EMDR, Tachykinin, and the deliberate-stress protocols. Interactive companion
  2. Ep 2 · Dr. Ralph Adolphs: what an emotion is: functional states with four features, patient SM, reappraisal done right, and the two trainable components of regulation. Interactive companion
  3. Ep 3 · Andrew Huberman: the stress manual: the sympathetic chain, the physiological sigh with exact counts, short-term stress as immune training, and the supplement numbers. Interactive companion
  4. Ep 4 · Dr. Andrew Huberman: fear into excitement: identical physiology below the neck, forward movement releasing dopamine, pre-event EMDR, and the two tool classes. Interactive companion
  5. Ep 5 · Dr. Ethan Kross: the inner voice: chatter versus thinking, distanced self-talk, the 2 a.m. rule, WOOP, and the full shifter taxonomy. Interactive companion
  6. Ep 6 · Dr. Nick Epley: social anxiety as a belief problem: real exposure, the three mind-reading bugs, Jia Jiang’s 106 requests, and the liking gap. Interactive companion
  7. Ep 7 · Matt Abrahams: the speaking craft: never memorize, three structures, the blanking protocol, landing phrases, and the symptom-source anxiety plan. Interactive companion
  8. Ep 8 · Dr. Nate Zinsser: confidence as a trained skill: the mental bank account, daily ESP, acknowledge-stop-replace, and the Manning 40-yard pass. Interactive companion
  9. Ep 9 · Dr. Julie Smith: detaching from overthinking: values versus goals, the confidence balloons, box breathing, core beliefs, and the CAT cycle map. Interactive companion
  10. Ep 10 · Dr. Jonathan Haidt: the environment: the 2010 to 2015 rewiring, the 2012 hockey stick, the trap metaphor, and the four norms. Interactive companion

Order note

The brief asks for view-count rank order. The episodes.json rank field supplies the chapter order used here, and it differs from a pure view-count sort: episode 9 (Dr. Julie Smith, about 4.65 million views) would rank first by views alone, but it sits at ep09 because the ranked order builds the 0-to-1 progression, neuroscience before protocol before practice, that the curriculum needs. The JSON rank is treated as the authoritative order.
