Hidden Brain: Track Index

2026-10-06 · Instructor: Shankar Vedantam · Hidden Brain

The track

Hidden Brain is Shankar Vedantam’s show about the unconscious patterns that shape human behavior. This track rebuilds its seven most-viewed episodes with available transcripts (ranked by verified YouTube views, October 2026) as textbook chapters: the full argument of each episode, numbers worked by hand, figures for every state change, and drills that train the mind.

This is track 12 of 14 in the AI Podcast Curriculum, and it sits at the center of the curriculum’s thinking arc. The arc runs across six tracks: models (Knowledge Project) → rationality (Clearer Thinking) → watching great minds (Tyler) → how minds work (Hidden Brain) → debugging self-delusion (YNSS) → sustained focus (Cal Newport). This track is the self-knowledge foundation: before you can reason well, study great minds, debug your delusions, or hold focus, you need to know how your own mind actually works. Its seven chapters cover the unconscious machinery: the instinct to add, the divided brain, the resilience you underestimate, the courage you misunderstand, the forgetting you need, the memorability code running under your taste, and the pressure that hijacks it all.

The intelligence-thread arc

The drills in “Think it yourself” get progressively harder across the track, by design. The first two chapters (ep01-ep02) give you guided drills with answer sketches. The middle chapters (ep03-ep05) remove the handrails: fewer worked answers, more journaling, field observation, and steelmanning. The final chapters (ep06-ep07) demand unaided reasoning from first principles: no answer sketches, design-your-own experiments, and a full track synthesis in ep07. This track continues the curriculum’s “reclaim your cognition” arc. Do every drill with your own brain before touching AI.

Episodes

Each line is why the episode matters: what it gives you that no other episode does.

  1. Ep 1 · Subtraction Neglect: the defect you cannot feel: your mind solves by adding even when removing wins. The 17-percent essay, the 2-of-90 recipe, the impossible itinerary, and the tools that force subtraction back in.
  2. Ep 2 · The Divided Brain: the real left/right science: both halves do everything, in different ways. The moral experiment that flips on intent, the mother’s arm that proves the left confabulates, and the master-emissary parable of the modern world.
  3. Ep 3 · Resilience: you are not as broken as you believe. Four trajectories, the 10-percent chronic ceiling, the five-stages myth dismantled, and the cost of the fragility script.
  4. Ep 4 · Courage: brave people are not fearless. The freeway rescuer’s three risks, the kidney donors’ flat generosity curve, the larger amygdala, and the reframe: do not ask how to be less frightened, ask how to love more.
  5. Ep 5 · Forgetting: perfect memory is a curse. Jill Price’s unfaded pain, memory as reconstruction, the city-grid model, the 89-versus-29 edit, and why your brain keeps the past that helps your future.
  6. Ep 6 · Memorability: the hidden code under your taste. The visual Mandela effect, the museum experiment beauty failed, and ResMem: an AI blind to art history that picks masterpieces from pixels.
  7. Ep 7 · Choking Under Pressure: the closing toolkit. Working memory as a small store, Cate Campbell’s hijacked final, the yellow square, the oath, and four tools: practice, reframe, breathe, ritual.

Honest note: three missing episodes

The track covers seven of the ten top-ranked episodes. Three episodes have no transcripts and therefore no chapters:

  • Rank 1: “The Real Reason You Can’t Focus (It’s Not Just Your Phone)” with Gloria Mark (54,278 views)
  • Rank 2: “Why Your Brain Sabotages Your Own Happiness” with Dave Evans (21,802 views)
  • Rank 8: “How to Stop Overthinking” with Ethan Kross (7,463 views)

All three have auto-generated captions on YouTube, but every timedtext download attempt returned HTTP 429 rate-limiting across four retry rounds, so no transcript could be secured from this network. The ranking was kept intact rather than substituting lower-ranked episodes, and no chapter was built from a title alone. If transcripts become available, they slot in as the track’s missing pieces: attention, happiness, and overthinking.
