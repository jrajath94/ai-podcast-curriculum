You Are Not So Smart with David McRaney: Track Index

2026-10-06 · Instructor: David McRaney · You Are Not So Smart

The track

You Are Not So Smart is the podcast about self-delusion: the psychology of motivated reasoning, bias, and belief. This track takes ten episodes and rebuilds each as a textbook chapter: zero-to-one progression, hand-worked numbers, figures on every state change, Q&A, memory aids, and drills done with your own brain before touching AI.

A note on sourcing

These episodes come from the show’s full 346-episode audio catalog, enumerated from the official RSS feed and ranked by substance for the track theme, not by popularity. Per-episode download counts are not public, so no view counts exist. Eight of the ten transcripts are interview-only transcripts from the show’s official transcript pages. David McRaney’s opening monologues are not included. Episodes 122 (Tribal Psychology) and 211 (QAnon) carry full-episode transcripts. Audio links are in each chapter’s frontmatter.

The thinking arc

This is track 13 of 14, and the debugging-self-delusion step of the cognition arc that runs across tracks 7, 10, 11, 12, 13, and 14: models (Knowledge Project) → rationality (Clearer Thinking) → watching great minds (Tyler) → how minds work (Hidden Brain) → debugging self-delusion (YNSS) → sustained focus (Cal Newport).

Knowledge Project gave you mental models. Clearer Thinking made you dangerous with them. Tyler showed you great minds at work. Hidden Brain showed you how minds work. This track debugs the machine: the judge is broken, objectivity is felt, beliefs are keystones, reason is social, and the debugger itself is constructed. Cal Newport, next, turns the debugged mind toward sustained focus.

The track’s own arc runs diagnosis → repair → field manual → the social machine → the deepest cut. Episodes 1 to 4 diagnose: broken self-judgment, false objectivity, identity keystones, reason as a social device. Episode 5 is the repair kit: Bayesian updating. Episode 6 names a new hole the repair must cover: outsourced knowledge. Episode 7 is the field manual: the one tested protocol for changing minds. Episodes 8 and 9 scale up: how communities and tribes capture the machinery. Episode 10 turns the instruments on their holder: the self is constructed.

The intelligence-thread arc

The drills in “Think it yourself” get progressively harder across the track, by design. Early chapters (ep01 to ep03) give you guided computations with answers shown: the buffer arithmetic, the sweet-spot predictor, the Monckton keystone test. The middle chapters (ep04 to ep07) ask for Fermi estimates and journaling with less scaffolding: expected-value forfeitures, door-knock math, self-catching protocols. The final chapters (ep08 to ep10) demand unaided reasoning from first principles: deriving the fringe-descent dynamic, designing falsification experiments, defining the self without circularity. Do every drill with your own brain before touching AI. This track’s drills are the hardest in the curriculum so far, because this is the debugging step: you learn to catch your own mind, not just to use it.

Episodes

Each line is why the episode matters: what it gives you that no other episode does.

  1. Ep 1 · The Dunning-Kruger Effect: the root bug: why the unskilled cannot tell they are unskilled, the double curse, the lemon-juice bank robber, and the buffers institutions build against your blindness.
  2. Ep 2 · Naive Realism: why you feel objective and your opponents feel biased: the “too” test, the bucket-of-facts fallacy, and the Douglass move for holding both grounds.
  3. Ep 3 · Belief: why facts do not work on people: the hero-maker brain, the sacred-emotion detector, and Lord Monckton’s life story as a load-bearing belief.
  4. Ep 4 · Arguing: reason did not evolve to find truth alone: the argumentative theory, why solo reasoning polarizes, the $50 coin flip, and why telling fails but arguing converts.
  5. Ep 5 · Bayes’ Theorem: the repair kit: grayscale beliefs, the two-worlds test for evidence, the 0.9-percent mammogram, and why your brain is not a Bayes net.
  6. Ep 6 · The Search Effect: the internet joined your transactive memory and you started counting its knowledge as yours: the nine experiments, the useless-search result, and the missing contractor’s buffer.
  7. Ep 7 · Deep Canvassing: the field manual: 12,000 doorstep conversations, 10 converts per 100, effects lasting months, and why nothing you say does the work.
  8. Ep 8 · QAnon and Conspiratorial Narratives: believers, not beliefs: the three-question test, the hourglass, the logic loop, and QAnon measured (not growing, not far-right, not big).
  9. Ep 9 · Tribal Psychology: the mainstream capture: minimal groups, affective polarization, math that bends for the tribe, and the HPV story about keeping science off the tribal track.
  10. Ep 10 · The Self: the finale: the debugger is constructed too: the half-second delay, the duplicator, the reflected self, and how to operate the instrument lightly.
