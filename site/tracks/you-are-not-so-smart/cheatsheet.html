You Are Not So Smart with David McRaney: Cheatsheet

2026-10-06 · Instructor: David McRaney · You Are Not So Smart

Dunning-Kruger (ep01 · David Dunning, 2014-11-10)

  • Definition: incompetent people are not in a position to know they are incompetent. Not denial, not self-deception.
  • Double curse: the same gaps cause mistakes and prevent detecting them. The judge is the defendant.
  • Self-assessment versus reality: relationship “relatively meagre, to often non-existent.”
  • MacArthur Wheeler: lemon juice on face for camera invisibility. Robbed Pittsburgh banks in daylight, no disguise. Caught within hours. Muttered “But I wore the juice, I wore the juice.” Tested with a Polaroid. Mis-aimed the camera.
  • Small pond: never saw the master level, so presumed near the top. College trauma: best in high school to bottom 20 percent.
  • Jeopardy uncle: counts only attempted questions, never the skips. American Idol: internal hearing differs from the room’s hearing.
  • Mediocrity enablers: face-to-face praise differs from behind-the-back talk.
  • Borderline: arrives quickly inside everyday life. We do not know where it is. Lemon-juice beliefs.
  • Geniuses do not know it: “if it is easy for me, it is easy for everybody.” Collective misses genius: Vertigo took about 50 years. Gettysburg address first called “short and uninspiring.”
  • Ehrlinger study: same test, “abstract reasoning” label drew 8 of 10 estimates, “computer programming” label drew 6 of 10. Top-down inference, not bottom-up experience.
  • Gender follow-up: equal performance, men estimated higher. Critcher follow-up: preconceptions reshape felt speed, familiarity, conflict.
  • General’s dial: doubting general before battle, confident general on the day. Confidence is a dial, not a virtue.
  • Institutions: devil’s advocates. Buffers: software estimates times 1.3, new OS up to times 2, architects multiply concrete by up to 8.
  • Embarrassment test: vaguely embarrassed by 5 to 10 years ago means evolving.
  • Dunning’s practice: accept wrongness fast, do not rebel against data, do not beat yourself up after.

Naive realism (ep02 · Lee Ross, 2015-11-05)

  • Definition: conviction you see the world objectively and unmediated. Reasonable others should agree. Disagreement is explained by their defect (ignorance, self-interest, culture, age).
  • Sweet spot: you are as reasonable as possible. Left is too idealistic, right is too callous. Ross: “if you thought it sensible to be more idealistic, you would be.”
  • “Too” test: too loud, too spicy, too extreme reveals your calibration, not the object.
  • Attribution asymmetry: your experience is enlightenment. Theirs is bias. Cheney’s daughter read both ways by side. Neutral sources look biased to both sides.
  • Einstein: “Reality is an illusion.” Umwelt (Eagleman): tick, fish, bullfrog, human. No privileged portrayal. Russell: noting a stone means noting its effects on you.
  • Oak tree versus bigot: shared hardware makes the tree safe. The bigot label rides unique life experience but feels identical.
  • Bias blind spot (Emily Pronin): the retina’s blind spot sits on the self.
  • Bucket-of-facts fallacy: paste links, expect conversion. Ross: 40 years of conflict work, zero people meeting the other side to be corrected.
  • Lord-Ross-Lepper: same two capital-punishment studies, both sides rated the agreeing one better done, both grew more certain.
  • Kahneman: “Every time a hypothesis fails, I gain 15 IQ points.”
  • Science’s one rule: interpret data through beliefs if you must, but never test beliefs on data you already interpreted through them.
  • End-of-history illusion (Gilbert): every life stage feels like the final accurate self. Ross at 20: linear progress. Later: pendulum, “struck the right balance.”
  • Fixes: reversed goal (try to be changed). Douglass move (hold your view and their ground). Ireland exercise (ask about community and family, not policy). Lincoln: dislike the man, then go know him better.

Belief (ep03 · Will Storr, 2014-09-30)

  • John Mackay: creationist, not stupid or mad. T-Rex teeth “so they can eat watermelons”. Outer space “so we can tell the time.” Facts impervious.
  • Better question: not “what is wrong with them” but “why do facts fail to persuade?”
  • Haidt: “If you want to find irrationality, look for the things that people make sacred.” Sacred equals emotional. He distrusts the left on climate and the right on markets for this reason.
  • Detector: heat above 7 disqualifies your brain. Storr will not tweet about Gaza. McRaney: write the reply, read it, delete it.
  • Hero maker: “we are basically all playing David in the story of David and Goliath.” Brain casts hero, villains, allies. Beliefs bend to the casting.
  • Beliefs unchosen: teen atheist could not decide to believe in God. Believers happier, less stressed (studies). “Who is the smart one?”
  • Religion: different in depth, not kind. God sits at the root of the cause-and-effect network.
  • Belief clusters: about 1,000 genes influence left-right via fearfulness. Fearful: structure, order, hierarchy, tradition. Open: novelty, cities. Self-reinforcing: Storr’s rudeness loop, corrected by his wife.
  • Lord Monckton: aristocrat, Harrow “ride and rule,” empire lost to WWII, welfare state blamed on the left, communists as jealous schemers, UN on the “KGB playbook” with a world-domination plan, himself as David. No fact can touch it: the belief is the keystone. Removing it collapses the identity.
  • “We are all Lord Monckton to a certain extent.”
  • Self-interrogation failure: “I know I am wrong about things” plus “I am not wrong about this, or this, or this.”
  • Twitter: public, visible to the admired, 140 characters strips the manners. “Almost custom designed to create massive rows.” Bushido code: post only what you love.
  • Balance: law at the harm boundary (homeopathy instead of medicine for a child means prison). Eccentricity as “the richness of our species” elsewhere. Dadaab warns against enforced sameness, religious or rationalist.

Arguing (ep04 · Hugo Mercier, 2013-09-27)

  • Argumentative theory (Mercier and Sperber, 2011): reason evolved to produce and evaluate arguments. Purpose: overcome trust bottlenecks (Sperber).
  • Two meanings of arguing: shouting versus exchanging reasons. Only the second.
  • Solo: confirmation bias, no sounding board, more polarized and confident. Goes “against the grain” to self-examine.
  • Group: truth wins. The right arguer convinces. Both can leave better.
  • NYT half-quote: “evolved to win arguments” drops evaluation. Losing with a better belief is the good outcome.
  • Bias is not always flaw: mushroom rule (bias toward poisonous is correct design).
  • Division of cognitive labor: “Rather than looking for flaws in our own arguments, it is easier to let the other person find them, and then only then adjust our arguments if necessary.” Restaurant example.
  • Justifiability over quality: Hsee chocolates. Small cheap heart enjoyed more. Big expensive roach picked because defensible.
  • Disjunction effect: coin flip plus $200 or minus $100. Winners flip again, losers flip again, unknown-outcome players decline. Expected value of the flip: plus $50, forfeited without a clean reason. Hawaii trip: pass buys (reward), fail buys (consolation), unknown buys nothing.
  • Sunk cost reframed: pressure to justify waste drives it. Children less susceptible. Taught economists carry the right justification. Context beats talent: “to reason well, it is much more important to be in the right context, than to be a good reasoner.”
  • Bat and ball: $1.10 total, bat $1 more. Intuition 10 cents (0.10 plus 1.10 is 1.20, wrong). Answer 5 cents (0.05 plus 1.05 is 1.10). Telling fails. 5 to 10 minutes of argument converts.
  • Abolition: arguments moved Parliament. Legal late 1700s to outlawed decades later. Internet optimism: printing survived its astrology panic.

Bayes (ep05 · Julia Galef, 2016-04-08)

  • Bayes’ rule: how probabilities should change with evidence. Beliefs are grayscale, 0 to 100, always moving.
  • Job hunt: confidence moves with each news item naturally. Ideology does not. The machinery is selective.
  • Credence: subjective probability cashed as betting odds. “67 percent” means “I take 2-to-1.”
  • Trick coin: probability is in the knower, not the coin. Your one half, Galef’s near zero after peeking, Data’s prediction: three probabilities, no contradiction.
  • Evidence defined: compare likelihood in the world where the hypothesis is true versus false. Hair: generic “looks good” is weak. Honest Jane’s is strong.
  • Car crash: even with a true excuse, crashes are likelier for bad drivers. Downgrade “at least a little.”
  • Bob and Amy: complaining fits jealousy and fits chronic complaining. Weak evidence. Ask the second question.
  • Breast cancer: 90 percent accurate test, 1 in 1,000 base rate. In 1,000: 1 true positive, about 100 false positives. Positive means under 1 percent (exact about 0.9 percent).
  • Plumber: looked up robbery base rates (low), moderate evidence, stayed in the Airbnb.
  • Fallacy of gray: “we cannot know” as shield. Run the two-worlds comparison. Accept lopsided answers.
  • Non-math habits: grayscale always. Everything is evidence. Small updates accumulate. Ask the honest question.
  • Bayes net: Harvard legacy example (legacy explains away IQ need). AI propagates instantly. Brains do not.
  • No Jenga block: flipping one node (same-sex marriage) leaves neighbors (God, LGBT-sin) untouched. Full propagation would paralyze thought. Argue nodes, not towers.
  • Source-check: trace challenged beliefs to their sources. Dead source means downgrade.

Search effect (ep06 · Matthew Fisher, 2015-11-19)

  • Cognitive tools: glove, writing, paper holding the birthday.
  • Transactive memory: old couple reconstructing better together. Each holds fragments plus addresses.
  • Division of cognitive labor: biologist trusts chemist and statistician.
  • Internet: ideal partner. Fast, mostly accurate, always available, one-way, asks nothing back.
  • “You erroneously include knowledge stored outside of your own head as your own.” Misattribution, not stupidity.
  • Nine experiments: internet group searches. No-internet group handed identical info from the same URLs. Self-rating on unrelated topics (weather): searchers rate higher.
  • Controls: time on task. Brain slider (barely lit to fully lit). “without outside sources” instruction. Effect survives.
  • Mechanism: filtered search (last week only, useless results) still inflates. It is the act of searching, not the learning.
  • Uniqueness: encyclopedia in the basement does not feel accessible. The pocket does.
  • When it matters: fine while the partner is present. Dangerous for the presidency and the voting booth, where judgment must be yours alone.
  • Confirmation pairing: finding confirming material gets easier exponentially. Next research frontier.
  • Contractor’s buffer: the trade prices underestimation into quotes. No knowledge buffer exists yet. Name it first.

Deep canvassing (ep07 · Dave Fleischer and team, 2016-07-13)

  • Prop 8 (2008): polls even, expected win, lost 52 percent against. “Let’s just go ask.”
  • Leadership LAB: about 1 percent of the $83M Center budget (about $830k/year).
  • 12,000-plus conversations. Recorded, reviewed, iterated. Discovered in the field, not designed from journals.
  • Fraud in first research batch. Broockman and Kalla got it retracted. Restarted blank-slate in Miami on transgender rights.
  • Trial: treatment (deep canvass) versus placebo (recycling chat). Hidden surveys at 3 days, 3 weeks, 6 weeks, 3 months.
  • Effect: 0.25 to 0.3 standard deviations. About 10 new supporters per 100 conversations. At least 3 months [trainer says up to 9]. Survives opposition ads.
  • 70 percent of opened doors finish the conversation. Training: 1.5 hours, pods, “puddles,” role-play.
  • Core rule: “There is nothing you can say, there is nothing you can tell this person that’s going to change their mind.” No talking points. Gut rejects facts “like an unhealthy virus.”
  • Reality over hypotheticals: the restroom stall, not the imagined transgender child.
  • Theory: System 2 (effortful) lasts. System 1 (ads) dies in a day. Petty and Cacioppo: effortful processing predicts durable change.
  • Analogic perspective taking: your memory of being judged becomes the bridge. Borrowed bridges do not hold.
  • A person changes their mind only by changing it themselves. Cooperative mystery-solving, no pressure.
  • Tree-bark stage: effect real, active ingredient unknown. Kitchen sink. Next research program.

QAnon (ep08 · Joseph Uscinski and Annie Sternisko, 2021-07-25)

  • Redirect: study believers, not beliefs. Content is least interesting.
  • Narrative over theory (Sternisko): cherry-picked, unfalsifiable, absorbs counterevidence.
  • Three-question test (1 to 7): secret plots run life. Few run things despite democracy. Real rulers unknown to voters. Most middle. Small slice high everywhere.
  • D&D analogy: the internet lets high scorers find each other.
  • Hourglass: fat top (profile) to bottleneck (one theory) to fat bottom (group identity). Fandom model: Alien via spaceships or via horror. Exit identity converges.
  • Content versus quality: social identity motive picks the plot (my group good). Uniqueness motive picks the genre (I am not a sheep).
  • Three base motives: feel good about self and groups. Make sense of the environment. Feel safe and in control.
  • Logic loop: counterevidence is cover-up. Missing evidence is hidden proof. No peer review. Contradictions coexist.
  • Collective sensemaking. Information voids (Kate Starbird): uncertainty plus anxiety fills voids with shared stories.
  • Distrust descent: update the interpretation, not the worldview. QAnon as last stop.
  • QAnon measured: not growing (thermometer 24 of 100, Florida 2018. 5 to 7 percent max). Not far-right (ideology drops out. Dark triad: narcissism, Machiavellianism, psychopathy. Plus institutional antipathy). Not big (rallies of 40 to 50. Hashtags are not believers).
  • Bar metaphor: collects the already-convinced. Beliefs predate QAnon by centuries.
  • Harm: 22 violent incidents in 3 years, none terrorism, 3 fatal. Versus 139 far-right non-QAnon, 34 religious, 37 far-left (CSIS). FBI 2019 bulletin: potential domestic-terrorism threat.
  • Mirage: Google alerts 5 to 50-100 articles a day. NYT 50 to 750 articles a year. Belief flat. Visibility is not growth. New thing is speed: lower community-formation barriers.
  • 45 percent endorse some JFK conspiracy. Half cannot name the plotter. Inclination without narrative.
  • Fix: empathetic dialogue over contempt. Meet the needs (belonging, validation, control). Communicate complexity honestly. Stop advertising the bar.

Tribal psychology (ep09 · Lilliana Mason and Dan Kahan, 2018-02-26)

  • Social identity theory (Tajfel, 1970s): loyalty defines self.
  • Minimal group: 40 dots, coin-toss labels (over/underestimators). Chose $4/$3 over $5/$5. Any label makes a tribe.
  • Brewer: need inclusion plus exclusion. Group status is self-esteem.
  • Affective polarization (Mason): dislike without disagreement. 92 percent of Republicans right of median Democrat. 94 percent reverse. 20 percent hardcore refusers. 27 versus 36 percent see the other party as a threat.
  • Party over policy: threatening the party angers more than threatening cherished positions.
  • Cohen welfare flip: label flip moves positions 100 percent. Subjects invent reasons.
  • Kahan, Linden: expert title granted, then revoked exactly when conclusions mismatch the tribe.
  • Gun-control math: math skill helps on skin cream always. On gun control it hurts whoever’s party the numbers oppose. Better math, better bending.
  • Iraq (Gaines): facts converged, interpretations diverged. Football film (Hastorf and Cantril): same film, 90 percent of Dartmouth blamed Princeton, Princeton saw half the infractions. Comey 2017: same hearing, exoneration versus condemnation.
  • Information deficit model: dead. 19th-century dream, never worked. Post-truth word of 2016 (2,000 percent spike). Kahan: he would like to know when this supposed truth era ran.
  • HPV versus HBV: 75 percent exposure in 20s/30s. 3,000 US cervical-cancer deaths a year. HBV 95 percent via doctors, no politics. HPV via Congress and cable news, tribal badge. Depoliticize before going public.
  • Fixes: science curiosity (the curious do not polarize with comprehension). Admit bias aloud, humanize per interaction (Mason’s drill). Share tribe-trusted sources only. “I am the problem. You are the problem. We all do this.” Fast food analogy: drives managed, not removed.

The self (ep10 · Bruce Hood, 2012-07-01)

  • Illusion: “an experience that is not what it seems.” Real experience, loose correspondence. Components: inhabiting body, coherence, individuality, free will.
  • Libet: conscious will arrives about 0.5 seconds after the brain prepares the act. The will is the press release.
  • Vision: central field only. Two tennis-ball blind spots. Blind during saccades. 2 to 3 hours of every waking day. Mirror test: cannot see own eye movements.
  • Duplicator: 5-year-olds accept copied toys. Insist copied hamsters have different minds. Stronger when named. Natural dualism (Descartes), untaught.
  • I versus me (James): I is raw awareness. Me is autobiography. Babies have I, not me. Infantile amnesia: nothing before about 2. Eastern children recall earlier (parents narrate sooner).
  • Function: I summarizes hidden processes (authorship feeling). Me runs the social game. Longest childhood installs the self.
  • Reflected self: identity negotiated outside. Ostracism triggers ingratiation. “Not myself” pleas, wine-blame: narrator patching continuity. Ware’s dying: number-one regret is a life lived by others’ reflections.
  • Materialism: mind is the brain’s product. Dementia proves it. “Meat machine” (Minsky). Complex-enough AI could be self-aware.
  • Twins: identical, about 50 percent alike in personality. Biology plus hall of mirrors.
  • Rebuild: sleep and anesthesia switch it off. Waking restores it. Dissonance patches decades.
  • Track close: the debugger is constructed. Operate the instrument. Do not obey the author. Three rules: audit by outcomes, read audience-shifts as data, choose your patches.
