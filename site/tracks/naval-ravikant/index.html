Naval Ravikant: Track Index

2026-10-06 · Instructor: Naval Ravikant · Naval Ravikant

The track

Naval Ravikant is the podcast about health, wealth, and happiness as practical philosophy. This track takes its ten most-viewed episodes (ranked by verified YouTube views, October 2026) and rebuilds each as a textbook chapter: zero-to-one progression, hand-worked numbers, figures on every state change, Q&A, memory aids, and drills done with your own brain before touching AI.

Research metadata note: episode lengths, rankings, and view counts on this page come from research/naval-ravikant/episodes.json (October 2026 snapshot). They are YouTube metadata, not claims from the episodes.

The spine of the track is the famous “how to get rich” tweet storm, expanded across a 3.5-hour mega episode and six short episodes: wealth versus money versus status, specific knowledge, the builder-seller pair, business models with leverage, luck, and judgment. Around that spine sit the two inner-game episodes: happiness as a skill and the 69-second monologue on copied desire. Plus the most tactical episode in the set: how to angel invest.

The thinking arc

This is track 6 of 15. The track runs in two movements. The outer game (ep01, ep03, ep04, ep06, ep07, ep08, ep09, ep10): how wealth actually gets created, from definitions to leverage to judgment. The inner game (ep02, ep05): why wealth alone does not satisfy, and how desire and happiness really work. The track’s final position: build wealth with the outer game, then discover it was not what you sought, and train the inner game directly.

The intelligence-thread arc

The drills in “Think it yourself” get progressively harder across the track, by design. Early chapters (ep01 to ep03) give you guided computations with answers shown: compound-interest tables, hourly-rate arithmetic, Metcalfe’s law checks. The middle chapters (ep04 to ep07) ask for Fermi estimates and journaling with less scaffolding: skill-stack combinatorics, copy-rate audits, power-law portfolio math, brand design. The final chapters (ep08 to ep10) demand unaided reasoning from first principles: variance models of luck, tipping-point derivations, and a full track synthesis written from memory. Do every drill with your own brain before touching AI.

Episodes

Each line is why the episode matters: what it gives you that no other episode does.

  1. Ep 1 · How to Get Rich: the full operating system in one chapter: wealth versus money versus status, equity over time, specific knowledge, accountability, three kinds of leverage, judgment, and the two-word summary, productize yourself.
  2. Ep 2 · Happiness: happiness as a trainable skill: desire as a contract for unhappiness, weaponized addiction, peace from mind, and the path to peace through truth instead of technique.
  3. Ep 3 · Wealth, Not Money or Status: the three definitions in six tight minutes: the sleep test for wealth, money as society’s IOU, status as zero-sum slot arithmetic, and how to spot a status bid.
  4. Ep 4 · Specific Knowledge: the only unfair advantage: knowledge that cannot be taught, found through genuine curiosity, recognized by the mother test, and stacked the Scott Adams way.
  5. Ep 5 · Your Desires Are Not Yours: 69 seconds on mimetic desire: most wants are copied from other people, and the practice of sitting alone to watch the mind and ask how you became like this.
  6. Ep 6 · Learn to Sell, Learn to Build: the two master skills in their broad forms, the Silicon Valley founder pair, and why one person holding both is unstoppable, sequenced build-first.
  7. Ep 7 · How to Angel Invest: the Spearhead playbook: three paths to equity, deal flow versus access, brand as the asset that buys access, pro rata mechanics, and the power-law math where one deal carries 99 zeros.
  8. Ep 8 · Making Money Isn’t About Luck: the four kinds of luck from blind fate to character-luck, the 999-out-of-1,000-universes standard, and wealth stacking one chip at a time.
  9. Ep 9 · Business Models With Leverage: structural leverage in the model itself: scale economies, zero marginal cost, network effects, Metcalfe’s law worked by hand, and the tipping logic of natural monopolies.
  10. Ep 10 · Judgment: the finale: leverage as a force multiplier for judgment, the Buffett and CEO-pay cases, 10,000 tries, the outrage test, and the pipeline from broad study to accountable calls.
