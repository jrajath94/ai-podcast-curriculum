Clearer Thinking with Spencer Greenberg: Track Index

2026-10-06 · Instructor: Spencer Greenberg · Clearer Thinking with Spencer Greenberg

The track

Clearer Thinking with Spencer Greenberg is the podcast about ideas that improve reasoning. This track takes its ten most-viewed episodes (ranked by verified YouTube views, October 2026) and rebuilds each as a textbook chapter: zero-to-one progression, hand-worked numbers, figures on every state change, Q&A, memory aids, and drills done with your own brain before touching AI.

The thinking arc

This is track 10 of 14, and the rationality step of the cognition arc that runs across tracks 7, 10, 11, 12, 13, and 14: models (Knowledge Project) → rationality (Clearer Thinking) → watching great minds (Tyler) → how minds work (Hidden Brain) → debugging self-delusion (YNSS) → sustained focus (Cal Newport).

Knowledge Project gave you mental models. This track makes you dangerous with them. It is the “reclaim your cognition” step: harder drills than Knowledge Project, aimed at the machinery of your own reasoning: memory, persuasion, noise, wisdom, motivation, conversation, institutions, charity, and finally rationality itself. The chapters train you to think, not just to know.

The intelligence-thread arc

The drills in “Think it yourself” get progressively harder across the track, by design. Early chapters (ep01 to ep03) give you guided computations with answers shown: forgetting-curve arithmetic, argument-count studies, tipping-point curves. The middle chapters (ep04 to ep07) ask for Fermi estimates and journaling with less scaffolding: noise audits, expected-value reasoning, disagreement triage. The final chapters (ep08 to ep10) demand unaided reasoning: mechanism designs, foundation-scale explicit models, paradigm shifts done from first principles. Do every drill with your own brain before touching AI.

Episodes

Each line is why the episode matters: what it gives you that no other episode does.

  1. Ep 1 · Andy Matuschak: memory as a cost problem you can engineer: the forgetting curve, the exponential backoff schedule, and why 12 seconds per card buys a year of recall.
  2. Ep 2 · Tyler Cowen: three portable laws for thinking plus the discipline of learning from people who offend you, and why raising aspirations may be the highest-return activity.
  3. Ep 3 · David McRaney: persuasion is self-persuasion: the elaboration likelihood model, the 3-versus-9 argument study, the affective tipping point, and the 17,000-conversation deep-canvassing protocol.
  4. Ep 4 · Daniel Kahneman: noise, the other half of error: the 208-judge audit, the 52-percent underwriters, three kinds of noise, and decision hygiene that works when debiasing does not.
  5. Ep 5 · Barry Schwartz: the case against the spreadsheet: incommensurable values, frames, constructed preferences, when intuition deserves trust, and the five trainable components of practical wisdom.
  6. Ep 6 · Julia Galef: the scout mindset: why motivation is the bottleneck, expected-value thinking from Bezos and Musk, and how to sound confident while staying calibrated.
  7. Ep 7 · Uri Bram: the mechanics of great conversation: building the tower together, why one in three disagreements is about definitions, marginal thinking, and why groups break at seven.
  8. Ep 8 · Robin Hanson: futarchy: vote on values, bet on beliefs, the worked CEO decision market, bill auctions with 5 percent to the proposer, and micro-trials you can run this month.
  9. Ep 9 · Marcus Davis: doing good effectively: the $3-a-day arithmetic, bed nets at a couple thousand dollars per life, the drowning-child arguments, and how to act under moral uncertainty.
  10. Ep 10 · Anna Riedl: the finale: the great rationality debate dissolved by resource-rational analysis, insight as representation upgrade, relevance realization, and wisdom as meta-rationality.
