Starship/Raptor: biggest time sink = ENGINE PRODUCTION, not design. "Prototypes are easy, production is hard." Raptor = full-flow staged combustion, very high chamber pressure. Key figure of merit: combustion chamber pressure → power density, thrust-to-weight, specific impulse (exhaust velocity = efficiency). Raptor designed for 300 bar (300 atmospheres); current operational record = Russian RD-170/180 at ~267 bar. Difficulty is nonlinear: 10% more chamber pressure ≈ 50% more difficult. Why hard to manufacture: very complex, many components, invented several new alloys; full-flow staged combustion has many recursive feedback loops (change one thing → recursive effects elsewhere) — "there's a reason no one's made this before." It's the highest theoretically possible efficiency — needed because fully reusable rocket = "the Holy Grail of orbital rocketry"; everything must be best: engine, airframe, heat shield, light avionics, mass shed everywhere. Example: catch booster/ship with tower chopsticks to save landing-leg weight — "catching the largest flying object ever made." "It probably won't work the first time."

First-principles method: 1) Physics is law, everything else is recommendation — check you're not violating physics (conservation of energy/momentum). 2) Boil to axiomatic base, reason up, cross-check conclusion against axioms. 3) Think in the LIMIT: scale to very large/small numbers (units manufactured, time). Manufacturing example: part expensive — is it foolish design or low volume? "What if we made a million units a year — still expensive?" If yes, volume isn't the issue; reduce design complexity. 4) Raw-materials "magic wand" number: weigh constituent elements (aluminum, steel, titanium, inconel, copper...), compute raw material value = asymptotic cost floor unless you change materials; then the ONLY reason it's expensive is how you arrange atoms into shape. In volume, cost asymptotically approaches raw material value + IP licensing. Most designers start from familiar tools/methods → inertia; instead imagine the Platonic ideal (perfect arrangement of atoms), then figure out what tools/materials get atoms into that shape. (Jim Keller "carries the flame" of this thinking at Tesla.)

Minimum tonnage ≈ at least a million tons (semiconductor fabs, iron refineries...). Mars is "the least inhospitable planet" but "definitely a fixer-upper."
