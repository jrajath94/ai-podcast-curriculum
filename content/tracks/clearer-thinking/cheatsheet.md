## Memory (ep01 · Matuschak)

- Forgetting curve is a power law: steep drop in first 24 hours, long flat tail.
- Spacing effect: same total time, spaced sessions retain more than one massed block.
- Exponential backoff schedule: 1 day, 1 week, 1 month, 1 quarter, 1 year.
- Review cost: 10 to 15 seconds per item per year, total (the episode's number), after four to five exposures.
- Active recall: pull the answer from memory instead of re-reading. Tests demand recall. rereading rewards recognition.
- The 10% question: what fraction of important ideas deserve cards? Sweet spot per book: 12 ideas, 30 prompt pieces, 4 kept.
- Quantum Country: mnemonic medium plus spaced repetition cost one third extra time for 6 months retention, one half for a year. Essays took about 4 hours each.
- Med students forget about one third of basic science after 1 year, about one half by end of year 2 [uncertain: episode attributes to "Custers" without full citation].
- Memory types: explicit (facts), implicit (skills), chess-master pattern memory (only in-domain).
- Prompt quality decides everything: behavior prompts, failure-mode cards, concept prompts. Atomized prompts rot.
- Para-academia: Patreon income near two thirds of an NSF CAREER grant. Full-stack social science: one person runs hypotheses to dissemination.

## Thinking laws (ep02 · Cowen)

- Law 1: something is wrong with everything. You do not understand a paper until you can state its flaw.
- Law 2: there is a literature on everything. Check before assuming novelty.
- Law 3: all propositions about real interest rates are wrong. Real rate = nominal minus inflation. Inflation 6.2 percent, nominal near 1 percent, real about negative 5 percent.
- Devalue and dismiss: one flaw found, status lowered, learning stopped. The sting is the signal.
- Offense triage: status threat means study them. real violation means note it and still study the view.
- Steelman by writing: 18 years of daily blogging. the best case for the other side, in public.
- Two universal advices: small peer group plus mentors. Mentorship is informal, periodic, concrete.
- Study art, music, literature, religion to crack cultural codes instead of sorting agree/disagree.
- Raising aspirations may be the highest-return activity: vividness through embodiment (Zuckerberg via Daniel Gross).
- Lookism: systematic mispricing of people by looks. short men show an earnings penalty.
- MMT: never coherently defined. one true claim (more room to monetize debt than thought).
- Behavioral economics: real effects, context-specific. loss aversion is not a fixed ratio.

## Persuasion (ep03 · McRaney)

- Persuasion is self-persuasion, not coercion. Create conditions. the mind changes itself.
- Belief answers to evidence. attitude to feelings and identity. values barely move. Bring facts to beliefs only.
- ELM: elaboration = motivation times ability. High elaboration takes the central route. low takes the peripheral.
- 3 strong vs 9 weak: engaged audiences prefer 3 strong. unengaged prefer 9 weak. Weak arguments hurt on the central route.
- Assimilation files new info under old categories. accommodation rebuilds categories. Minds default to assimilation.
- Motivated reasoning: conclusion hires its reasons. Fluency is the tell.
- Moral dumbfounding (Haidt): strong judgment, justifications exhausted. Confabulation: smooth invented reasons (split-brain).
- Affective tipping point (Redlawsk): around 15 percent counter-evidence doubt shows. around 30 percent updating starts. below it, backfire.
- Deep canvassing: 17,000 conversations. Rapport, consent, shoulder-to-shoulder, claim plus 1-to-10 number, "why does that number feel right," story, counterfactual, accept any movement.
- Street epistemology: for fact beliefs. claim, their definitions, confidence 0 to 100, evidential standards.
- Topic rebuttal for shared-rule settings. technique rebuttal for conversations. Ethics: truth and autonomy only.

## Noise (ep04 · Kahneman)

- Error squared = bias squared + noise squared. Bias is systematic. noise is scatter.
- 208 judges, 16 cases: two random judges differ by about 4 years on a 7-year average sentence. Judge lottery.
- Underwriters: expected 10 percent difference, measured 52 percent. Five times the expected noise.
- Level noise (average harshness differs), pattern noise (feature weights differ, usually biggest), occasion noise (mood, lunch).
- Noise audit: independent re-judgment of shared cases, measure scatter, decompose, match fix.
- Model of the judge beats the judge: keep the knowledge, drop the bad mornings.
- Fragile Families: best models moved prediction from about 50 to about 57 percent. Know the ceiling.
- Decision hygiene: mediating assessments (score parts separately), independent judgments before discussion, intuition last.
- Premortum: imagine the judgment failed, explain why, adjust, average the two. Two estimates from one head.
- Planning fallacy: inside view fails. reference-class forecasting (outside view) anchors on base rates.
- Groups polarize without structure. averaging independent judgments cancels noise. Crowd within helps.
- Kahneman doubts debiasing. trusts procedure. Replication crisis was self-deception. Bayesian updating is the norm.

## Wisdom (ep05 · Schwartz)

- Rational choice = expected utility spreadsheet. Needs complete options, numbered values, stable preferences. Real decisions offer none.
- Counting replaces thinking: numbers feel rigorous, inquiry stops. Ask what the numbers leave out.
- Incommensurable values: no common currency. forcing one distorts.
- Frames: Pollan beef externalities. COVID narrow framing hid school and livelihood costs. Reframe twice.
- Preferences are constructed during elicitation. the spreadsheet measures what measuring creates.
- Kahneman-Klein: trust intuition when environment is regular AND feedback is fast. Firefighters yes, stock pickers no.
- Carlsen: tell me when to think. Pattern positions play themselves. spend the clock on the critical few.
- Wisdom components: reflect on aspirations, fit across life, judgment, openness, it-depends.
- Three bricklayers: bricks, wall, cathedral. Same work, different meaning.
- Rules need judgment: applying any procedure is itself a judgment. Train the hand, not just the tools.

## Scout mindset (ep06 · Galef)

- Soldier defends, scout maps. Counter-evidence feeling like attack means the soldier drives.
- Bottleneck is motivation: CFAR (2012) taught great techniques. students did not use them.
- Rational irrationality (Caplan): epistemic vs instrumental rationality. False beliefs can pay. price the trade consciously.
- Present bias in thinking: effort now, payoff later, so the mind skips. Belief ripple effects: one belief anchors a cascade.
- Bezos: base rate 10 percent, personal estimate 30 percent, EV = 0.30 times huge payoff. Acted on the product, not certainty.
- Musk: 10 percent each for Tesla and SpaceX, assumed failure, acted on mission plus EV.
- Social vs epistemic confidence: "85 percent" is honest and sounds competent. Bezos told investors 70 percent failure odds.
- Reframe truthfully (survives evidence) vs self-delusion (needs protection from facts).
- Press secretary spins. board governs. Report to the board.
- Monkey Island: lose the duel, keep the rejoinder. Bet beliefs to operationalize them.
- Jerry Taylor: soldiers change sides through common ground plus better maps.

## Conversation (ep07 · Bram)

- Tower: lay bricks together, climb higher than either alone. No new bricks, no tower.
- Cached thoughts: too fast, too smooth, does not touch what you said. Ask for the unthought thought.
- Performance debate: would they say it with no audience? Speed matching: meet their pace.
- Vibe first: goodwill is the precondition. Voice-only can free attention from monitoring load.
- One in three disagreements is definitional. Taboo the word. restate without it.
- Secret debates: definitions hide in "is it," values hide in "is it good." Surface both. map values to consequences.
- Marginal thinking: not good/bad but what the next unit buys. Immigration: the question is the margin.
- Dark forest vs garden: subconscious as forest beyond the wall. feed it, consult it. Nine muses (playful).
- Light gassing: warm agreement reinforcing false perceptions. opposite of gaslighting.
- Groups: 4 works, 7 breaks (buzzer problem). Set relevance and interruption thresholds explicitly.
- Be the host (Tamsin Wolf): draw out the quiet, redirect the dominant, land the ending.

## Futarchy (ep08 · Hanson)

- Vote on values, bet on beliefs. Democracy mixes both jobs. voters are rationally ignorant, bettors get paid.
- Conditional decision market: trades called off unless the decision is taken. price estimates outcomes under that decision.
- CEO example: market A (called off if CEO stays) prices value-if-fired. market B prices value-if-kept. Higher price wins.
- Decision selection bias: read prices at decision time with decider info in the market.
- Insider trading welcome in decision markets: accuracy is the product.
- Subsidized market makers: pay for information when the decision is worth it. Combinatorial markets price interactions.
- Welfare function: the hard voted part. fight it once, openly.
- Bill auction: propose, market judges, passes automatically if positive, 5 percent of gains to proposer.
- Hypocrisy tax: support a ban, bet the ban works (prostitution example).
- Trials: restaurant darts, ship-date bets, hiring markets. Markets die where managers feel threatened. start below the radar.
- Sacred money: spendable only on sacred things (health, education, charity), through a sacred bank that verifies the end. tax career agents (20 percent income auction). cost-accounting equilibrium.

## Giving (ep09 · Davis)

- $3 a day: global poor income. Poor American: $25,000 to $30,000 after taxes, 80th to 95th global percentile.
- Bed nets: dozens of RCTs, about a couple thousand dollars per life saved. Demand trials over testimonials.
- Drowning child (pond): distance does not matter. River: a million miles, a million children, no stopping point. Demandingness: live in the tension.
- Within-a-mile altruism (Parfit): defend your boundary or move it.
- Incomparability is a dodge: the trade-off happens anyway. make it explicit.
- Explicit models: write assumptions, numbers, spreads. GiveWell spreadsheets. All models wrong, some useful.
- Monte Carlo: distributions not points. Ranges are honesty.
- Moral uncertainty: reflective equilibrium iterates theory vs judgments. Aggregate by weighted average or budget split (50 percent credence = 50 percent weight or budget).
- $1,000 goes to GiveWell: best explicit model, not perfect. Charity feedback loops are weak. supply the discipline.

## Rationality (ep10 · Riedl)

- Axiomatic (von Neumann/Morgenstern axioms [uncertain: transcript says "Loyman and Morgenheim"]) vs ecological (fit to environment) rationality.
- Resource-rational analysis: thinking costs time. standard is accuracy per cost. Biases as speed-accuracy trade-offs.
- Meta-rational calculation: is further thought worth it? Keep the test cheap.
- Sunk cost survives deliberation: fast errors are economics, slow errors are motives.
- Nine dots: the box was never there. insight is dropped assumption.
- Domino: 64 squares, each domino 1 black + 1 white, opposite corners same color, 32 vs 30 remain, 31 dominoes need 31/31. Impossible. Representation beats search.
- Procedural over declarative: own the moves, not the lists.
- Relevance realization (Vervaeke [uncertain: transcript says "Vivekis"]): what to attend to. emergent loop of goals with world.
- Wisdom as meta-rationality: no theory of fitness, only evolution. no fixed rationality, only adaptation.
- Typical mind fallacy: inner lives differ wildly, outer behavior copies. Ask, do not assume.
- Paradigms: behaviorism (Skinner, black box) to cognitivism (information processing). Two lenses humble.
- Simon: a diagram is sometimes worth 10,000 words. Stuck? Redraw first, rethink second.
