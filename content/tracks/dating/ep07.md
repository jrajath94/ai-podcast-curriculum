# Episode 7: 13 Semi-Controversial Truths About Men & Women

Guest: Adam Lane Smith, attachment specialist and author. This episode aired 2025-07-24 and ran 4955 seconds.

The episode makes one claim: attachment is not a therapy topic. It is the operating system of partner choice. Learn to read it, provide safety at four levels, and vet for mission before fun.

## The episode in one paragraph

Smith frames the relationship as a company: the man is the CEO, the woman is the COO and co-executive. The wrong woman costs him his goals as he manages problems she refuses to fix. The right woman is a jet engine strapped to his back. His core instrument is the four levels of safety a man must provide: physical, resource, emotional, and bonding, the last one biochemical, oxytocin and vasopressin signals that he will not leave. The dating market numbers are stark: 65 percent of Gen Z is insecurely attached against 35 percent secure, and the secure minority marries young and exits the pool. Avoidant men stuck in war mode suffer a measurable biochemical cascade, blocked oxytocin receptors, no GABA, unsuppressed cortisol, bad sleep, low serotonin, falling testosterone, and the fix is nervous-system integration with a safe woman, which the episode says extends lifespan by 5 to 15 years. Porn and video games sedate men out of status-seeking entirely, consuming about 8 years of the average American man's life. The episode closes with the divorce correction, the vetting rules, and the thesis that male loneliness is cured by masculine intervention, not feminine intervention.

## The company frame and the four levels of safety

Start with the frame because everything hangs on it. The man is the CEO. The woman is the COO, a co-executive, not an employee and not the boss. Pick the wrong COO and the CEO spends his life managing her unfixed problems instead of running the company. Pick the right one and she is a jet engine strapped to his back: the same man, multiplied.

The CEO's job description is the four levels of safety. Level one is physical: she is safe in her body around you. Level two is resource: finances handled, problems spearheaded before they reach her. Level three is emotional: you receive her problems graciously, stay emotionally disciplined, and do not blow up. Level four: bonding. You are biochemically bonded to her, and your oxytocin and vasopressin signaling tells her nervous system you will not leave.

Level four has a precondition most men miss. She must be able to receive safety. Four generations of women were taught not to trust men, so many cannot take in what a good man provides. Safety offered to a system that cannot receive it reads as pressure or control. Vet for receptivity, not just for your own performance.

## The pool numbers and the oxytocin cascade

The market statistics set the difficulty. Among Gen Z, 65 percent are insecurely attached and only about 35 percent are secure. Personality disorders rose from 10 percent to about 20 percent. Smith's summary line: "Modern dating is what happens when the 35 percent of securely attached people get married young and leave the pool and the other 65 percent try to manipulate each other into shared stimulation."

Then the biochemistry, the episode's most technical passage. Avoidant men get stuck in the sympathetic nervous system, war mode, and cannot reach the parasympathetic, rest and digest. Blocked oxytocin receptors start a cascade: without oxytocin signaling, the body cannot synthesize GABA, which normally suppresses cortisol, so stress runs unsuppressed. Poor melatonin production follows, so sleep degrades. Low serotonin follows, so mood drops into depression. Testosterone begins to diminish around age 30 to 35. Erectile dysfunction appears around age 30 to 35. Novelty-dopamine relationships die at 5 to 7 months. High risk of cancer, heart attack, and stroke begins around age 40 to 45. An honesty note: the transcript gives the 30-to-35 and 40-to-45 ranges with no unit. They read as ages from context, but the speaker never says the word age [uncertain]. The Iceland saying Smith quotes: "Bare is the back of a brotherless man."

The fix is nervous-system integration, and it runs through her. A safe woman pulls the avoidant man into parasympathetic mode: oxytocin rises, GABA rises, serotonin rises, testosterone rises, human growth hormone rises. Her oxytocin is contagious. The episode's headline number: intimacy with the right woman extends his lifespan by about 5 to 15 years. The female nervous system needs the same safety to maintain fertility and bonding. This is physiology, not poetry.

A note on the red-pill passage, because the episode names it directly. Smith says red-pill dating "encourages anxiously attached men to act avoidantly attached to prey upon anxiously attached women," and traces the teaching style to disorganized attachment, often via borderline mothers, behind many red-pill teachers. Read it as the episode's claim, not as settled science: the mechanism it describes is real enough to recognize, perform avoidant to trigger anxious, whatever the theory of its origins.

## Sedation, pity, and the masculine answer

The male sedation hypothesis, credited to Williamson, asks why sexless young men are not rioting. History says they should: young male syndrome, Portugal in the 1800s, first sons marry while the rest ship out on galleons, predicts antisocial behavior from mateless males. The answer is sedation: porn and video games numb men out of status-seeking and reproductive behavior. The price tag is time: the average American man spends 8 years of his life in escapist entertainment. Smith's line: we are comforting men to death.

Validation without accomplishment completes the trap. Telling a stuck man he is great as he is does not register as love. It registers as pity, like being a charity case, and the episode links the burden-feeling to suicide in older men. The solution for stuck men is other men: reintegrate the male network, mentor rather than pity or bootstrap. "The answer to male loneliness is not feminine intervention, it is masculine intervention." Women cannot mentor a man out of a hole that male respect fills.

The attraction mechanics follow from attachment, not from game. Avoidantly attached men have the appearance of masculinity: distance reads as strength. Anxiously attached men get friend-zoned: need reads as weakness. Smith's line: "the friend zone is almost exclusively caused by anxious attachment in a man." Fix the attachment and the zone dissolves without a single new line.

## Vetting: divorce, mission, and the simple life

The divorce statistic gets corrected. The 50 percent rate is a lie built by counting every marriage including the seven-marriage people. First marriages succeed at about 65 to 70 percent. Couples who pray together daily carry under 1 percent divorce risk, but Smith reads the cause as shared mission, purpose, principles, culture, and openness, not religion as such. Arranged marriages show higher happiness and lower divorce. The secular extraction: united purpose, agreed right and wrong, a marriage conduct contract beyond the prenup, and the regular sharing of joys, hopes, sorrows, and fears.

Vasopressin is the bonding mechanism for men, who carry more of its receptors. Accomplishing things together builds loyalty, mate-guarding, and protectiveness. Hence the vetting rule: "find a woman who is mission focused, not fun focused." Fun fades. Mission compounds.

Love gets a definition built for vetting: "a series of continuous actions and choices for the person's well-being." Affection is a feeling. Love is a practice. And the fear line: "People are not afraid of love, they are afraid of losing it." The closing operating principle: "Your life does not need to be made easier, it needs to be simpler."

## How to imbibe this

This week, vet like a CEO. First, score your current or prospective relationship on the four safety levels, one paragraph each, with evidence. Second, test her receiver: name one recent moment you offered safety and describe how she received it. Third, audit your sedation: log your hours in porn and video games for seven days, multiply by 52, and face the yearly number. Fourth, contact one man: a mentor, a friend, a group, and make it recurring. Fifth, write your mission in one sentence, then ask whether your dating life serves it or escapes it. Simpler, not easier.

## Think it yourself

Brain first, no AI. The drills now ask you to diagnose systems, not just people.

1. A couple fights weekly about money. Map the fight onto the four safety levels: which level is actually breached? What would fixing that level look like, in one concrete action?
2. A friend is deep in porn and games, lonely, and defensive. The episode forbids pity and prescribes masculine intervention. Script your first conversation: what do you say that is neither pity nor bootstrap lecture?
3. She is fun, beautiful, and bored by your mission. He is plain, steady, and lit up by it. Run the CEO/COO hiring decision with the episode's criteria. Who gets the offer, and what is the cost of the other choice?
4. The 50 percent divorce rate is cited at dinner. Deliver the correction with the episode's numbers, then give the secular version of the under-1-percent recipe.
5. Design a weekly ritual that builds vasopressin: one shared accomplishment, repeatable, with visible progress. Explain why it bonds more than a fun date does.
6. "Your life does not need to be made easier, it needs to be simpler." Apply it to your dating life: name three things to remove, not add.

## Memory aids

Mnemonic: **P-R-E-B, receiver on.** Physical, Resource, Emotional, Bonding. Then check her receiver.

Never-confuse pairs:
- **CEO vs COO.** He sets direction. She runs operations. Either one doing the other's job is a failing company.
- **Sedation vs rest.** Rest restores you for the mission. Sedation removes the mission. Eight years tells you which one you chose.
- **Pity vs mentoring.** Pity says you are fine as you are. Mentoring says you can be more and I will walk with you. Men hear the difference instantly.
- **Fun focused vs mission focused.** Fun is a mood. Mission is a direction. Vet for direction.

Trap cards:
- **The 50 percent trap.** The divorce rate counts serial marriers. First marriages succeed at 65 to 70 percent. Price marriage with the right number.
- **The avoidant-mask trap.** Distance looks like masculinity. It is often just avoidance with good posture. Do not hire the mask.
- **The red-pill trap.** Performing avoidant to trigger anxious is manipulation wearing strategy clothes. The episode names the attachment damage underneath.
- **The easier trap.** Optimizing dating for ease adds apps, filters, and sedation. The episode says optimize for simple: mission, standards, reps.

## Q&A

**Q1. Explain like I am new: what are the four levels of safety?**
They are the four things a woman's nervous system needs from a man before it can relax into a relationship. Physical: her body is safe with you. Resource: money and problems are handled, you go first at hard things. Emotional: you can hear her worst day without blowing up or shutting down. Bonding: your chemistry, oxytocin and vasopressin, tells her you stay. Each level opens the next. And she has to be able to receive them, or the whole ladder scores zero.

**Q2. Why does the episode say modern dating is 65 percent trying to manipulate 65 percent?**
Because the secure 35 percent marries young and leaves. The pool left behind is mostly insecurely attached people, and insecure systems relate by managing the other person's reactions: the anxious manage by pursuing, the avoidant by withdrawing. "Shared stimulation" is Smith's cold phrase for what passes as chemistry down there: two nervous systems keeping each other activated. It feels like passion. It is dysregulation with a good story.

**Q3. Walk through the oxytocin cascade in plain order.**
Blocked oxytocin receptors mean no GABA gets synthesized, so cortisol runs unsuppressed and the man lives in war mode. Bad sleep follows from low melatonin, low mood from low serotonin, testosterone begins to diminish around age 30 to 35, erectile dysfunction appears around the same ages, novelty relationships burn out in 5 to 7 months, and high risk of cancer, heart attack, and stroke begins around age 40 to 45. Open the receptors through safety and every arrow reverses: oxytocin, GABA, serotonin, testosterone, and growth hormone rise, and lifespan extends 5 to 15 years. One receptor state, two completely different men.

**Q4. Applied: you suspect you are sedated. Run the episode's audit.**
Log every hour of porn and video games for one week. Multiply by 52 for the yearly figure and divide your age into the 8-year average to see where you stand. Then ask the young-male-syndrome question: what status-seeking would you do if the sedation were gone? Name one concrete pursuit, career, craft, or competition, and schedule its first hour this week. The audit is not moral. It is arithmetic: 8 years is the price, and the receipt is in your screen-time log.

**Q5. What is wrong with comforting a stuck man by telling him he is great?**
He hears pity, not love. Validation without accomplishment tells him you graded his performance and found nothing to grade, so you grade his existence instead. The episode says men experience this as being a charity case, and links the burden-feeling to the darkest outcomes in older men. The alternative is masculine intervention: another man who has done it, mentoring him toward something real. Respect is the medicine. Comfort is the sedative.

**Q6. How do the divorce numbers change the marriage decision?**
They change the base rate. The scary 50 percent counts every remarriage, including people on marriage seven. Your relevant number is first marriages: 65 to 70 percent succeed. Then the under-1-percent recipe: shared mission, agreed values, a conduct contract, and regular sharing of inner life, with daily prayer as the religious wrapper. The decision becomes vetting-shaped: find mission alignment first, and the statistics move to your side before the wedding.

**Q7. Why "mission focused, not fun focused," and how do you test it?**
Because vasopressin bonds through shared accomplishment, not shared amusement. A fun-focused woman optimizes the evening. A mission-focused woman optimizes the decade, and the decade is where loyalty, protectiveness, and devotion compound. Test it early: propose something hard and shared, a project, a goal, a build, and watch whether she lights up or checks out. Her reaction to effort is the whole vetting interview.

## Skills you can now use

- **Safety audit.** Score any relationship on the four levels with evidence, and test whether her receiver is on.
- **Attachment reading.** Classify anxious, avoidant, secure, or disorganized from behavior, and name the loop before you join it.
- **Sedation accounting.** Convert screen hours into life years and read the number without flinching.
- **Vetting interviews.** Probe for mission focus, receptivity, and shared values before commitment, with the divorce base rates in hand.
- **Masculine intervention.** Mentor or recruit mentorship among men: direct, respect-based, pity-free.
- **Simplification.** Remove three complications from your dating life and name the mission the simplicity serves.
