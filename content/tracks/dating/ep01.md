# Episode 1: Only four ways: How to meet women

Guest: Orion Taraban, a psychologist who works on men's mental health and runs the PsycHacks channel. This episode aired 2025-04-21 and ran 1069 seconds.

The episode makes one claim: there are only four ways to meet women, and they trade ease against success rate in a straight line. The easy ones barely work. The hard ones work well.

## The episode in one paragraph

Taraban says a man can meet women through four channels only: dating apps, cold approach, social circle, and social media. He ranks them from the easiest and fastest, which have the lowest success rates, to the hardest and slowest, which have the highest success rates. Dating apps cost nothing to start but filter most men out before a woman ever sees them. Cold approach works but demands hundreds of attempts and the confidence to survive them. Social circle takes at least six months to build and sharply limits who you can date. Social media has the highest ceiling but asks for years of audience building, and attention is the currency that buys everything in it.

## The four channels, ranked

Start from zero. A man wants to meet women. Where can he go? Taraban says the list has exactly four entries, and the order never changes. From easiest to hardest: dating apps, then cold approach, then social circle, then social media. From lowest success rate to highest: the same order, reversed.

### Dating apps: free to start, brutal to win

The best thing about dating apps, Taraban says, is that a man can get started for free in about twenty minutes and, with the right system, build a consistent pipeline in a couple of weeks. The worst thing is the success rate. For an average man it is very low.

The episode draws two lines in the sand. If a man is an eight or above in pure physical attractiveness, with emphasis on the face, he can "absolutely clean up" on the apps. Taraban is blunt about why: for such men, apps become a non-stop funnel of new enthusiastic women, and nothing else about him matters much. Everyone else needs to be at least a six to see "any appreciable success."

Then come the filters. If a man is under 5 feet 7 inches tall or older than 50, the app will show his profile to almost no women. It will keep showing him women's profiles, which gives him a false sense of hope. Taraban stresses that women will date shorter men and older men in real life. But on the app, sitting at home imagining an ideal man, with the interface telling them they are in a seller's market, their stated standards drift away from what they would accept in person.

The price of this channel is honest: a false sense of hope. The app shows a man women he will never reach. He reads the parade of profiles as demand for him. It is demand for the men who pass the filter.

### Cold approach: the volume game

Cold approach means walking up to a woman you have never met and starting a conversation. Four problems come with it. First, stranger danger: women are wary of unknown men, for good reasons. Second, success depends on snap judgments of your current presentation, with no context to speak for you. Third, it is scary. Fourth, the numbers are brutal.

Taraban says men generally need a system plus an accountability structure to beat approach anxiety, and they will likely need at least a few hundred approaches before they feel remotely confident. He adds that even the best pickup artists in the world have success ratios that read like baseball batting averages: they strike out more often than they get a hit.

Work the arithmetic. A batting average of .300 means 3 hits in 10 at-bats, and 7 strikeouts. The world's best are below .300. Suppose a man runs 300 approaches, the minimum confidence threshold from the episode, at a .200 "batting average": 300 times 0.20 equals 60 positive outcomes, and 240 rejections. That is the honest shape of the channel. Most men will also need to approach "scores" of women, meaning dozens, for the channel to make sense at all.

The hinge question of this section: if even the best fail most of the time, what separates the men who win at cold approach from the men who quit? Answer: the men who win expect the rejections. They budget for them like a cost, not a verdict.

### Social circle and social media: the slow builds

Social circle means meeting women through friends, work, hobbies, church, and the friends of friends. The success rate is high, because time and context do the selling before you do. A woman meets you vouched for, and she can watch you over weeks before anything starts.

The price is time and narrow choice. Taraban says it takes at least six months to make a new friend or feel belonging in a new place, and only with real effort. And your options shrink to the pool you can reach: date only inside your church and the candidate list may hold a dozen names.

Work the calendar. Start today, 2026-10-06, and the earliest honest date for a built circle is around April 2027. Any plan that needs a social circle this month is a plan that started six months too late.

Social media is the last channel and the strangest. It is the hardest to build and the highest-yielding when it works. The mechanism: a man posts a curated view of his life, and women respond to the lifestyle proposal. Taraban's line: "the ability to command attention makes men irresistible to women." Men expand their reputations through visible competence. His example: there are only 6 percent of YouTube channels with over 100,000 subscribers, against 51 million channels total, which he says gives roughly 300,000 successful creators.

Check that arithmetic, because it is a trap card. 51,000,000 times 0.06 equals 3,060,000, not 300,000. The guest's two numbers do not close. The number that closes with 300,000 is 0.6 percent: 51,000,000 times 0.006 equals 306,000. Either he meant 0.6 percent, or he divided wrong. The direction of his claim survives, success is rare, but the figures as stated cannot both be true.

Social media also has a gender asymmetry the episode states flatly: it is far harder for men than for women, because the currency is attention and the market for male attention-seekers is crowded.

### The inverse law: easy in, little out

Put the four together and the pattern is one line. Apps: start in 20 minutes, lowest success. Cold approach: start this week, low success, high fear. Social circle: start in six months, high success, narrow pool. Social media: start in years, highest ceiling.

The honest price of the whole episode: there is no free lunch in meeting women. Every channel charges in a different currency, time, fear, patience, or public effort. Men who refuse all four currencies stay single and call it bad luck.

## How to imbibe this

This week, do three things. First, score yourself honestly on the app question: rate your face a number from 1 to 10 as a stranger would, and check the 5-foot-7 and age-50 cutoffs. If you are below a 6, below 5 foot 7, or over 50, treat apps as a lottery ticket, not a plan. Second, pick one slow channel and put a calendar date on it: join one recurring group activity this week, and write down the date six months out when the circle will start paying. Third, catch yourself in the false-hope loop: when you catch yourself scrolling profiles of women who cannot see you, close the app and log one approach attempt or one social outing instead. Attention is the currency. Spend yours where it buys something.

## Think it yourself

Do these with your brain first, no AI, no notes from this chapter. Early in the track the drills are guided. They get harder.

1. From memory, list the four channels in order from easiest to hardest. Then write one sentence for each: what currency does it charge?
2. You are 5 foot 6, age 34, face a 7. Which channels does the episode recommend, and which does it rule out? Answer before you check the chapter.
3. The guest says 6 percent of 51 million channels is roughly 300,000 creators. Do the multiplication yourself. What percent actually gives 300,000? Write the arithmetic.
4. A friend says cold approach is a waste because the success rate is low. Use the batting-average argument to answer him in three sentences.
5. Design your own week: you have five free hours. Split them across the four channels and justify the split with the inverse law. Then ask: does your split match what you actually did last week?

## Memory aids

Mnemonic: **ACCS, easy to hard.** Apps, Cold approach, Circle, Social media. Say "acts easy to hard" and you have the order.

Never-confuse pairs:
- **Filter vs preference.** Women will date shorter and older men in real life. The app filter is not their preference. Do not read the filter as a verdict on you.
- **Seeming vs being.** In ep09 Taraban adds: the person with means who does not seem to possess them gets approached no more than the person who lacks them. On apps, the photo is the seeming. In a circle, your life is the seeming.
- **6 vs 8.** Six in face is the floor for appreciable app success. Eight in face is the level where apps become a funnel. Floor and ceiling are different numbers.

Trap cards:
- **The 300,000 trap.** 6 percent of 51 million is 3,060,000, not 300,000. If you repeat the guest's figure, pair it with the correction: the consistent percent is 0.6.
- **The hope trap.** The app shows you profiles of women who cannot see you. Scrolling them feels like a market. It is a shop window.
- **The confidence trap.** Confidence in cold approach comes after a few hundred attempts, not before. Waiting to feel ready is the same as quitting.

## Q&A

**Q1. Explain like I am new: what is the whole episode about?**
A man can meet women in only four ways: apps, cold approach, social circle, social media. They line up from easiest to hardest. The order of success rates is the exact reverse. So the man who picks the easiest channel gets the worst results, and the man who pays the highest price gets the best. The episode is a pricing menu. Pick your currency: speed, fear, patience, or public work.

**Q2. Why does Taraban say apps have the lowest success rate even though they are the easiest?**
Three reasons stack. First, the filter removes men under 5 foot 7 and over 50 before any woman judges them. Second, everyone left competes on photos alone, so only men around a 6 in face or above get real traction, and only 8s and above get a flood. Third, women on apps act as if they are in a seller's market, so their stated standards run hotter than what they accept in person. Easy entry for men plus filtered visibility equals a lottery.

**Q3. A friend says: "Cold approach is dumb. Even the best guys fail most of the time." What is the error?**
He treats the failure rate as a reason to avoid the channel instead of as the channel's price. The episode says the best in the world fail most of the time, like baseball batting averages. The correct response is arithmetic, not avoidance: budget 300 attempts, expect most to miss, and count the hits. The men who win at cold approach are not the men who rarely fail. They are the men who planned for failure.

**Q4. Applied: you move to a new city on 2026-10-06 and want a dating life by spring. Build the plan.**
Do not rely on social circle: six months of building puts payoff around April 2027, which misses spring. Run apps in parallel but treat them as a lottery unless you clear the face and filter cutoffs. Put the main effort into cold approach, a few hundred attempts across the winter, plus one recurring group activity started now so the circle matures for later. The plan matches each channel to its real timeline instead of hoping the fast ones behave like the slow ones.

**Q5. What does "the ability to command attention makes men irresistible to women" mean in practice?**
It means the currency of social media is not looks or money, it is captured attention. A man who can hold a room, a feed, or a stage gets approached. That is why men expand reputations through visible competence: the competence must be seen to count. The practical read: post proof of what you can do, not claims about who you are.

**Q6. Why is social circle high-success but still a bad primary plan for many men?**
Two constraints. Time: at least six months to build belonging, so it cannot serve a man who needs results now. Scope: you can only date inside the pool you can reach, and a small pool means few candidates. High success rate times tiny volume can still lose to a low success rate times huge volume. Rate and volume multiply. Never judge a channel by rate alone.

**Q7. What is the "false sense of hope" mechanism, exactly?**
The app shows a filtered-out man the profiles of women who will never see him. His brain reads the stream of attractive profiles as evidence that the market is full of options for him. The options are real. His access to them is not. The feeling of hope comes from one side of the transaction being hidden.

## Skills you can now use

- **Channel audit.** Score any man's situation on the four channels: app viability (face estimate, height and age cutoffs), cold-approach volume (can he do 300 attempts), circle maturity (how many months deep), social-media attention (does anyone watch him). Name the strongest channel and the missing one.
- **Timeline math.** Given a start date, add each channel's build time and state the payoff date out loud. Six months for a circle, years for an audience, weeks for an approach habit.
- **Hope-loop catch.** Notice when app scrolling replaces action, and convert the session: close the app, log one real-world attempt.
- **Arithmetic check.** Multiply any "percent of a big number" claim before you repeat it. 51,000,000 times 0.06 is 3,060,000. Trust the product, not the speaker.
