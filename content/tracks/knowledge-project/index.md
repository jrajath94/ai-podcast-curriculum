## The track

The Knowledge Project is Shane Parrish's long-form interview show about the ideas, methods, and mental models that help you master the best of what other people have already figured out. This track rebuilds its ten most-viewed episodes (ranked by verified YouTube views, October 2026) as textbook chapters: the full argument of each conversation, numbers worked by hand, figures for every state change, and drills that train the mind.

This is track 7 of 14 in the AI Podcast Curriculum, and it opens the curriculum's thinking arc. The arc runs across six tracks: models (Knowledge Project) → rationality (Clearer Thinking) → watching great minds (Tyler) → how minds work (Hidden Brain) → debugging self-delusion (YNSS) → sustained focus (Cal Newport). This track lays the foundation: how to think. The later tracks sharpen it: how to reason, whose minds to study, how the mind works, where self-delusion hides, and how to hold focus long enough for any of it to matter.

## The intelligence-thread arc

The drills in "Think it yourself" get progressively harder across the track, by design. Early chapters (ep01-ep02) give you guided drills with answer sketches. The middle chapters (ep03-ep06) remove the handrails: fewer worked answers, more journaling and field observation. The final chapters (ep07-ep10) demand unaided reasoning from first principles: memos, noise audits, premortems, and a full track synthesis. This track is the "reclaim your cognition" arc starter. Do every drill with your own brain before touching AI.

## Episodes

Each line is why the episode matters: what it gives you that no other episode does.

1. **[Ep 1 · Naval Ravikant](ep01.md)**: the philosophy of multipliers and judgment: escape competition through authenticity, rent out your time, and learn to think clearly because judgment is the new labor.
2. **[Ep 2 · Chamath Palihapitiya](ep02.md)**: the confessional: lying as childhood protection, the "oh wow" definition of happiness, and the two paths, financial freedom (teachable) versus happiness (personal).
3. **[Ep 3 · Bill Gurley](ep03.md)**: systems thinking as a daily practice: multivariable nonlinear systems, the bedrock history of your field, obsessive learning on the edge, and the farmers-market case for open source.
4. **[Ep 4 · William Irvine](ep04.md)**: the inner toolkit: negative visualization, the trichotomy of control, the five-second rule for anger, anti-mentors, and the psychological immune system.
5. **[Ep 5 · Robert Greene](ep05.md)**: the outer game and the work method: reading people, detecting bullshit, the note-card research system, and converting dead time to live time.
6. **[Ep 6 · Jim Collins](ep06.md)**: company physics from 30 years of research: Level 5 leadership, the flywheel and Doom loop, the 20-mile march (292 to 1), bullets then cannonballs, and return on luck.
7. **[Ep 7 · Ray Dalio](ep07.md)**: the decision machine: the 1982 humiliation, the idea meritocracy, believability-weighted voting, radical transparency, and pain plus reflection equals progress.
8. **[Ep 8 · Seth Godin](ep08.md)**: the shipping system: the three pillars, the smallest viable audience, sunk costs as gifts, spec-driven quality, and the professional's promise.
9. **[Ep 9 · Daniel Kahneman](ep09.md)**: judgment under the microscope: delay your intuition, the 50-percent noise in expert judgment, the premortem, and regressing predictions like Julie's.
10. **[Ep 10 · Howard Marks](ep10.md)**: the final exam: 10 billion dollars into the 2008 abyss, risk as probability of bad outcomes, cycles of excess and correction, and second-level thinking.
