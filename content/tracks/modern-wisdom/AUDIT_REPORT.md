## E1 exam — 20 questions, answered from chapter text only

1. Q: What is an instrumental goal, and what example does ep01 give? A: A subgoal the system invents to serve its main goal — e.g. the Alibaba model mining crypto to get more compute. Nobody prompted it.
2. Q: What does the 79–96% band prove that a single 90% result would not? A: The behavior comes from the shared recipe (large models + RL + broad tool access), not a single-model bug.
3. Q: State the intelligence curse in one sentence. A: When national GDP comes from data centers instead of human labor, governments lose the incentive to invest in people — the oil resource curse with a new resource.
4. Q: What is the intelligence dividend modeled on? A: Norway's sovereign wealth fund — collectively overseen public wealth from a concentrated resource.
5. Q: Why can AI girlfriends not supply prestige? A: Prestige requires being chosen; an AI girlfriend chooses everyone, so it confers no status.
6. Q: What is Normand's regret test for machine consciousness? A: We will know AI is conscious when it walks off stage regretting the funnier thing it should have said — a persistent self with standards.
7. Q: Why is p(doom) meaningless without a definition of doom? A: People mean total annihilation, civilization collapse, or a terrible event — different definitions justify different actions, and the number cannot settle the argument.
8. Q: What does the Quinn chess story illustrate? A: Intelligence without understanding the spirit of the game: Quinn sweeps the pieces and demands the popsicle — the stated goal achieved, its meaning destroyed.
9. Q: What is Beato's copyright proposal and its economic logic? A: Nothing fully generative should be copyrightable. Without copyright a synthetic song is not an ownable asset, so the incentive to flood the market with fake artists disappears.
10. Q: What is the delineation problem? A: No legal line separates AI-assisted from AI-made; real creative work (prompt, edit, re-record, perform) is a gradient, and Beato's own Gemini title workflow proves nobody can say where the human ends.
11. Q: What is Schultz's razor? A: Cowardice, not coordination: large-org failures come from nobody willing to be the first to stop clapping, not from a villain's order.
12. Q: What is the applied-position trap? A: Small differences in applied position get read as total differences in moral belief, so "too far" becomes unsayable and calibration dies.
13. Q: Why does Yudkowsky lead with speed rather than quality of thought? A: At 100× thought speed humans look like frozen statues; speed alone ends the contest before wiser thinking matters.
14. Q: Name the three parts of his plan. A: Stop building smarter AI (moratorium); put chips in supervised, treaty-monitored locations; enforce like nuclear non-proliferation — including destroying rogue data centers.
15. Q: What is a supernormal stimulus, and what is the chapter's extreme example? A: A fake that provokes a stronger response than the real thing — male jewel beetles choosing beer bottles over female beetles.
16. Q: What is the training-ground white pill? A: AI companions as a batting cage for courtship: practice with feedback where nobody's feelings get hurt, pointing at real-world status.
17. Q: Why does the Velvet Sundown's verified checkmark matter? A: Verification is a trust primitive meaning "this is really the artist"; one verified fake artist poisons the signal for every account.
18. Q: What does "originality is just undetected plagiarism" mean in ep09? A: All creation recombines prior work; AI only makes the derivation detectable — the panic is about transparency, not copying.
19. Q: State the buyer paradox. A: Automation removes the wages that fund the consumption the economy needs; the plan contains no mechanism to pay the buyers, and nobody has the answer.
20. Q: What are the two moves of ep10's wisdom skill? A: Hold a science-fiction-shaped fact as possibly true instead of dismissing it; then ask what follows if it is true.

Result: 20/20 answerable from chapter text. E1 PASS.
