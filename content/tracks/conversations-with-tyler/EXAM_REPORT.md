### Chapter 1 — Malcolm Gladwell (ep01)

**Q1 [NUM].** The endowment chapter claims Gladwell's alternative is to
spend to zero instead of the 5-percent perpetuity. Start with $1
billion and spend 5 percent of the remaining balance each year.
(a) Compute the balance after 30 years and the percent spent.
(b) Compare the endowment's cumulative 20-year payout with the
concentrated $50 million-per-year-for-20-years spend-to-zero path. By
what factor does the concentrated path deploy more money in the first
20 years?

Answer: (a) Balance after n years is 1B × 0.95^n. 0.95^30 ≈ 0.2146,
so about $214.6M remains and about 78.5 percent is spent. This sits
between the chapter's stated 64 percent after 20 years and 99 percent
after 100 years (ep01:144). (b) The endowment's 20-year cumulative
payout is 1B × (1 − 0.95^20) ≈ 1B × (1 − 0.3585) ≈ $641.5M. The
spend-to-zero path deploys the full $1B in the first 20 years, so it
concentrates about 1,000 / 641.5 ≈ 1.56 times more money in the first
20 years, and finishes the job instead of smearing payouts across five
generations (ep01:144, ep01:321).

**Q2 [ELI].** Explain like I am new: what is Tyler's five-part model
of Malcolm Gladwell, and what did Gladwell add to correct it?

Answer: The five parts are: (1) the postwar Caribbean Enlightenment
tradition (Wynter, James, Fanon) via his mother Joyce Gladwell, giving
science-as-liberation plus contingency and optimism; (2) a Mennonite
streak from his Canadian childhood, meaning people can be good;
(3) Canadian modesty; (4) a mathematician father, giving respect for
data; (5) 10,000 hours at the Washington Post, giving data-based
storytelling. The themes that fall out are contingency, optimism,
voluntarism, and the power of the individual (ep01:29-47). Gladwell's
correction: Tyler named only positive influences. Gladwell added the
negative influence, a trait formed by reacting against your
surroundings: his family were not storytellers, so he became one to
fill the role. His mother writes on paper the stories she would never
tell in public (ep01:53).

**Q3 [APPLIED].** You work at a nonprofit university that caps
enrollment while sitting on a large endowment. Run Gladwell's
luxury-handbag test on it, then run the Klarman question on a reform
proposal to triple enrollment.

Answer: The handbag test (ep01:122-144): is the school in the
education business (goal: educate as many as possible, expand) or the
luxury business (goal: sustain brand equity, treat expansion as
dilution)? Capped enrollment plus a $40B-scale endowment matches the
Louis Vuitton pattern, not the education pattern. The reform audit:
the Rosenwald alternative is spend-to-zero in 10 to 20 years at
$50M-$100M a year on the actual problem (ep01:144). Then the Klarman
question (ep01:280): before pushing the reform, ask whether the
backlash is likely to be 10X the reform. The Brown backlash distorted
Southern politics for two generations and dwarfed the decision
itself. If tripling enrollment invites a donor revolt, rank collapse,
or trustee coup larger than the reform, design the smaller version.

### Chapter 2 — Camille Paglia (ep02)

**Q4 [NUM].** Rebuild Paglia's rejection ledger and compute two
ratios: (a) rejections per year over the book's gestation, and
(b) the share of her life-to-date that the 20-year project consumed.

Answer: 7 publishers plus 5 agents = 12 professional rejections over
20 years of work, published at age 43 (ep02:45). (a) 12 / 20 = 0.6
rejections per year, or roughly one rejection every 1.67 years.
(b) 20 / 43 ≈ 46.5 percent of her life to publication. The point the
chapter draws: the ratio of labor to recognition is the argument, and
the response function is infuriation, not discouragement
(ep02:45, ep02:288).

**Q5 [ELI].** Explain like I am new: what is Paglia's theory of
culture cycles, and how does the Baroque-to-Rococo example work?

Answer: "A culture always moves in cycles" (ep02:74). Periods that
esteem the colossal alternate with periods that esteem the small.
The Bernini Renaissance and Baroque, big swirls and big gestures,
evaporate into the Rococo, "little tiny things like on a Valentine's
card." Hollywood's giant 1950s screens, built to fight television,
evaporate into tiny smartphone screens. Each phase evaporates into
the other, and the wheel always turns back (ep02:74-82).

**Q6 [APPLIED].** A headline says a group earns 80 cents on the
dollar. Apply Paglia's three-layer decomposition from the pay-gap
section before you moralize.

Answer: Layer one, the slogan: 72 to 75 cents is an average across
different jobs, not same-job pay; calling it same-job pay is "the lie
they tell" (ep02: pay-gap section, ep02 Figure 5). Layer two, the
averages: women take more flexible jobs, avoid around-the-clock and
road-heavy commission sales, prefer closeness to children, so the
mix drives the average. Layer three, the root: biological differences,
including the mother-infant tie (ep02 Figure 5). The applied move:
name the mix effects first, then argue about the moral. The chapter
adds the weakest point as a follow-up: the audience member's own
proposal, if flexibility demand came from both sexes, the margin
penalty would shrink without denying the biology (ep02 Q&A).

### Chapter 3 — Stephen Kotkin (ep03)

**Q7 [NUM].** A district holds 10,000 hectares of arable land.
Voluntary collectivization in 1928 covered 1 percent. How many
hectares were voluntarily collectivized, how many had to be taken by
force to reach 99 percent, and what is the forced-to-voluntary ratio?

Answer: 1 percent of 10,000 = 100 hectares voluntary, the land of
people who could not farm alone (ep03:146). To reach 99 percent,
9,900 hectares must be collectivized in total, so 9,800 by force.
The ratio is 99 to 1 (ep03:158). The chapter's conclusion: the policy
was not persuasion scaled up; it was confiscation (ep03:248).

**Q8 [ELI].** Explain like I am new: what is the difference between
censorship as suppression and censorship as marination?

Answer: Suppression removes information. Marination promotes certain
information and ways of thinking until they feel like common sense.
Magnitogorsk residents did not just lack facts about the West; they
had a full alternative picture they were marinated in: Western workers
never on holiday, resorts only for the bourgeoisie, Reagan a
warmonger (ep03:112). Propaganda is a diet, not just a locked door
(ep03 Q&A on censorship).

**Q9 [APPLIED].** Your company proposes a machine-learning system to
fix a broken process without changing who holds power. Run Kotkin's
cybernetics-fantasy test on it.

Answer: The test (ep03 cybernetics section, ep03 Figure 2): the
planned economy was a fantasy, never a reality; cybernetics was a
fantasy in a different light, promising better information so the
regime would not have to reform. Kotkin says the Chinese Communist
Party runs the same play today: technology instead of structural
reform, to keep central control and the monopoly. Ask of your ML
proposal: does it remove the need to give up power? If yes, suspect
the fantasy. Name the structural reform it avoids. The honest version:
the scientists can be world-class and the problems genuinely
fascinating while the politics stay self-serving (ep03 Q&A).

### Chapter 4 — Peter Thiel on Political Theology (ep04)

**Q10 [NUM].** Thiel identifies anti-communism from 1949 to 1989 as
the katechon that restrained communism. How many years did it run?
Compare that span to the 75-year Civil War backlash and the 25-year
Brown backlash from Chapter 1, as ratios.

Answer: 1989 − 1949 = 40 years (ep04:219-221). Ratios: 40/75 ≈ 0.53
of the Civil War backlash, and 40/25 = 1.6 times the Brown backlash
(ep01:280). The katechon then morphed into neoliberalism and world
governance, which is the chapter's recursive danger: the restrainer
becomes the thing (ep04:221, ep04 Figure 4).

**Q11 [ELI].** Explain like I am new: what is the katechon, and why
does Thiel say it can become the Antichrist?

Answer: The katechon is the mysterious biblical restraining force
that holds back the one-world totalitarian state. Never identify it
with an institution (ep04: katechon section, ep04 Figure 4). The
Girardian reason it can flip: the restrainer gets mimetically
entangled with the thing it restrains. Claudius restrained Nero, but
both were Roman emperors. Anti-communism restrained communism, then
morphed into neoliberal world governance. Greta as climate katechon
demands total enforcement. The rule: "Does not the Antichrist just
come to power by acting as a katechon?" (ep04 Figure 4).

**Q12 [APPLIED].** Take a political coalition you belong to and run
the Reagan-dinner test on it.

Answer: Seat its three types at dinner, the millionaire, the general,
the priest in the 1980s version, and find what they actually have in
common (ep04: Schmitt section). Thiel's answer for the Reagan
coalition was one thing: anti-communism, a common enemy. Then ask:
what happens the day the enemy falls? The coalition disintegrated
after 1989, which is the natural Schmittian analysis (ep04).
Then apply Thiel's warning about Schmitt: is politics a market that
works better when the friend-enemy split is understood, or a
scapegoat machine that breaks once seen? Describing the witchcraft
village can accelerate the disintegration (ep04).

### Chapter 5 — Peter Thiel on Stagnation (ep05)

**Q13 [NUM].** Life expectancy rose 2.2 to 2.5 years per decade since
1840. (a) How many decades separate 1840 from the 2015 interview?
(b) What total gain does the rate imply over that span?

Answer: (a) 2015 − 1840 = 175 years = 17.5 decades. (b) 17.5 × 2.2 =
38.5 years; 17.5 × 2.5 = 43.75 years. The chapter adds Thiel's own
extrapolation: early to mid-90s straight-line, 100 to 110 in the
upper case, with recent decades' gains coming from people in their
60s and 70s living longer (ep05:166).

**Q14 [ELI].** Explain like I am new: what is hysteresis in this
episode?

Answer: A history of failure discourages, so failure begets failure.
"No halfway sane parent would encourage their kids to study nuclear
engineering today," while crowds push into software (ep05:39).
Success draws talent, which draws innovation, which draws talent:
the cycle runs both ways. The hope is that signal successes in the
world of atoms flip the vicious cycle into a virtuous one. Precedent
is the mechanism (ep05:39, ep05:281).

**Q15 [APPLIED].** A funded project in your company keeps getting
money despite a basic physics problem. Run the Solyndra test on it.

Answer: The test (ep05:200): a cylinder's surface area is 2 pi r
versus 2 r for a flat panel, so by ninth-grade geometry the cylinder
was 1-over-pi as efficient and never commercially viable, yet a
Nobel laureate ran the department that funded it. Write the
one-paragraph, middle-school-level calculation that kills the
project. If nobody was allowed to do it, name who benefits from the
prohibition. Thiel's rule behind it: a government (or company) that
cannot use ninth-grade geometry on its own investments "should not
be allowed to make any investments in these areas whatsoever"
(ep05:200).

### Chapter 6 — Jhumpa Lahiri (ep06)

**Q16 [NUM].** Lahiri lived in Rhode Island from age 3 to 18. In
1961, at age 14, she found the Earhart article and spent three years
researching her. (a) How many years did she spend in Rhode Island?
(b) In what year did the three-year Earhart research end, and how old
was she?

Answer: (a) 18 − 3 = 15 years (ep06:31). (b) 1961 + 3 = 1964, at
age 17. The research made her "a feminist before feminism revived":
the Earhart generation of 1920s-30s career women, who demanded equal
opportunity without bad-mouthing men (ep06: research section).

**Q17 [ELI].** Explain like I am new: how did Lahiri translate six
Bengali short stories if she cannot read Bengali script?

Answer: The taped-translation method. One, her mother reads
Ashapoorna Devi aloud in Bengali. Two, Lahiri tapes her. Three, she
replays the tapes until the sound lives in her ear. Four, she
translates from sound, checking the text painstakingly where she
can. Five, she catches skipped paragraphs and phones her mother
about them (ep06:255). She could read the script slowly enough to
check structure and breaks. The general principle: translate from
your strongest channel, not the official one. Sound beat script
(ep06 Q&A).

**Q18 [APPLIED].** Apply the uniform-versus-costume test to one
"cover" in your own life, literal or professional.

Answer: The uniform (Reclam's plain German covers, a school uniform)
is protective: plain, series-bound, says nothing about you (ep06
Figure 5). The costume (the US trade cover, the floating tiara
designed from her bracelet) forces a statement about your identity
that you did not choose: "Once the cover is on it, it is not my
book." The childhood root: the $40 Nike sneakers her parents called
a waste, and the dream of waking up in a blue skirt, white shirt,
and black shoes with nobody judging her clothes (ep06 Figure 5).
List your covers, mark which you chose and which were assigned, and
price the assigned ones.

### Chapter 7 — Steven Pinker (ep07)

**Q19 [NUM].** (a) If 90 percent of languages may die, leaving about
600, how many languages exist today? (b) Snuck is about 120 years
old in this November 2016 episode. Around what year did it get its
toehold?

Answer: (a) 600 / (1 − 0.90) = 6,000 languages (ep07:212). Spanish,
Hindi, Russian, and Chinese are safe, and translation software plus
national pride prevent one global tongue. (b) 2016 − 120 ≈ 1896
(ep07:68). Snuck survived by parasitizing the memory strength of
phonological neighbors, dig-dug and stink-stunk.

**Q20 [ELI].** Explain like I am new: what are Pinker's two machines
of language?

Answer: Machine one is rote memory: arbitrary sound-meaning
pairings, stored by brute force, like the word duck, which looks
nothing like a duck (ep07: Two machines). Machine two is the rule
system: algorithms that compute meaning from parts, like adding -ed
to walk to get walked (ep07:33). Irregular verbs are the war zone:
about 165 memorized exceptions (sing, sang) versus the algorithm
for everything else. Rare verbs fall below the memory threshold
and children default to the rule: dreamt becomes dreamed (ep07:35,
ep07 Figure 2).

**Q21 [APPLIED].** Your company just renamed a failing initiative.
Run the euphemism-treadmill test on it.

Answer: The treadmill (ep07 Figure 4): negro was respectful through
the 1970s, then black, then African American from the 1990s. Attitudes
stain labels, not the reverse. Rename without moving the attitude and
the new label absorbs the old taint. It holds only where prejudice is
weaker, as with Asian replacing Oriental. Ask: did the attitude move,
or only the name? Demand evidence of the attitude. Note also the
grammar boundary: politically motivated change works on open-class
words, not on closed-class items learned early, so watch whether the
rename touches structure or only branding (ep07: treadmill section).

### Chapter 8 — David Deutsch (ep08)

**Q22 [NUM].** Deutsch's decision theory says the proportion of the
multiverse doing one thing plays exactly the role probability plays
in a randomness theory. A transporter kills you painlessly in 1
percent of worlds and delivers you in 99 percent. How should that
1 percent move your decision, compared with a classical 1 percent
failure chance?

Answer: Identically. Proportion replaces probability, so the 1
percent of failing worlds counts exactly as a classical 1 percent
risk would (ep08:37). "A few worlds where something happens should
not move your decision if the proportion is tiny." The fork changes
nothing else: both versions of him are error-correcting entities,
and that is what human thought is (ep08:35). He steps in, but not
first (ep08: transporter section).

**Q23 [ELI].** Explain like I am new: what is the Zeus wall?

Answer: Comprehensible does not mean fully understood; it means no
barrier. A claimed barrier to knowledge is logically equivalent to
the supernatural: past the wall, things are exactly as if Zeus
decided them, and what is beyond affects us anyway (ep08: Zeus wall
section, ep08 Figure 3). Simulation theory without testable effects
is "Zeus with computers": indistinguishable from Zeus or Odin by any
argument. If it came with GPU access and weird testable effects, it
would be an empirical theory. Godel fits the same frame: no solid
foundation for all knowledge, yet rules of inference are conjectures
and we still really know things (ep08 Figure 3).

**Q24 [APPLIED].** Apply the squirrel test to one routine of yours.

Answer: The squirrel on bare concrete performs the full hind-leg
scrabbling and nudging burial routine to zero effect: "just a
program being enacted by its genes" (ep08:123). The gorilla repeats
the useless thumb motion every time. Both want the outcome; neither
knows why each action serves it. Humans differ: we act on
explanations, so our chapter 37 is blank. Write the explanation you
act on for your routine. If you cannot write it, you are the
squirrel. Stop the routine until you can state the explanation, and
then check whether the explanation is true (ep08 Q&A).

### Chapter 9 — Any Austin (ep09)

**Q25 [NUM].** (a) In a group of 500 self-described gamers, how many
buy a game in a given year? (b) If Austin's channel reaches 2,000
new viewers, about how many arrive via the YouTube algorithm?

Answer: (a) 12 percent of self-described gamers buy a game in a
given year (ep09:116), so 500 × 0.12 = 60. (b) About 90 percent of
his discovery is the YouTube algorithm (ep09:29), so about 1,800.
Everything else is the margin. The rule: algorithm equals audience;
Instagram effort buys disproportionately little reach (ep09 Figure
1).

**Q26 [ELI].** Explain like I am new: what is Austin's ideal-form
move?

Answer: Step one: judge the game's pole against the ideal pole.
Step two: judge the real pole against the ideal pole. Step three:
notice reality fails the test too. A utility pole with the wire
wrong looks broken in the game, but walk outside and real
infrastructure is usually wrong versus the ideal: the guy who was
supposed to dig the hole, the contractor with his own constraints
(ep09 Figure 2). Both are builds with glitches. The move dissolves
the superiority of the real over the simulated: glitches reveal the
build either way.

**Q27 [APPLIED].** Someone in your field says the craft is in
decline. Run Austin's graph test on the claim.

Answer: "Tell them to do it, and then I will look at the graph"
(ep09). Games are not getting better because better is the wrong
axis after the nascent period: after the basic tools are figured
out, art gets different and more expensive, not qualitatively better.
Red Dead Redemption II versus 1984 Zelda: different timbre, same
impact on the heart (ep09 Figure 3). The checklist: demand the
graph; check whether the complaint is better-versus-worse or
different-versus-same; ask who benefits from the decline story
(ep09 Q&A). No graph, no claim (ep09:222).

### Chapter 10 — Niall Ferguson (ep10)

**Q28 [NUM].** Collingwood's method is put best in his 1939
autobiography. The Ferguson interview is dated 2021-07-28. How many
years separate the two?

Answer: 2021 − 1939 = 82 years (ep10:96). Ferguson found it after
crossing the Atlantic to Harvard: "sudden illumination." Against it
stands the modern ethos of condescending to the past with our values
and calling that insight (ep10:96).

**Q29 [ELI].** Explain like I am new: what is Collingwood's method?

Answer: The historical act is the reconstitution of past thought
from such relics as survive, juxtaposed with the thought of your own
time (ep10:96). The steps: (1) gather the relics of past thought;
(2) reconstitute their thought; (3) juxtapose it with your own
time; (4) ask what light it sheds on your predicament (ep10:109).
Forbidden: studying the past for its own sake, and condescending to
it with modern values. The past is an instrument for seeing the
present, not a trophy shelf (ep10 Figure 3).

**Q30 [APPLIED].** Take your most unfashionable belief and run the
Empire audit on it.

Answer: The method (ep10 Figure 4): cost-benefit against realistic
counterfactuals, not against utopia or the Third Reich. Ferguson's
heresy, held 20 years: the benefits of the British Empire outweighed
the costs, because the 19th-century empire ran free trade, free
migration, free capital, and abolition, and built more infrastructure
than the indigenous alternatives, while failing primary education
badly (ep10:133, ep10 Figure 4). Your audit: name the realistic
alternative, price both sides, admit the failures (his: no primary
education; Roy's India book is the honest source), and concede the
method if you cannot run the counterfactual. No counterfactual, no
verdict (ep10 Q&A).
