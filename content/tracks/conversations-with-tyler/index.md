## The track

Conversations with Tyler is the podcast where Tyler Cowen interviews great minds at full speed. This track takes its ten most-viewed episodes (ranked by verified YouTube views, October 2026) and rebuilds each as a textbook chapter: zero-to-one progression, hand-worked numbers, figures on every state change, Q&A, memory aids, and drills done with your own brain before touching AI.

## The thinking arc

This is track 11 of 14, and the watching-great-minds step of the cognition arc that runs across tracks 7, 10, 11, 12, 13, and 14: models (Knowledge Project) → rationality (Clearer Thinking) → watching great minds (Tyler) → how minds work (Hidden Brain) → debugging self-delusion (YNSS) → sustained focus (Cal Newport).

Knowledge Project gave you mental models. Clearer Thinking made you dangerous with them. This track shows you the models running inside great minds: how Tyler asks questions, how his guests build their production functions, how mental models look in action. You do not just learn the ideas. You watch Gladwell notice, Paglia categorize, Kotkin historicize, Thiel theologize, Lahiri translate, Pinker mechanize, Deutsch unify, Austin see, and Ferguson counterfactualize. The next tracks turn inward: how minds work, how they deceive themselves, how to focus them.

## The intelligence-thread arc

The drills in "Think it yourself" get progressively harder across the track, by design. Early chapters (ep01 to ep03) give you guided drills with answers shown: Fermi estimates, noticing exercises, category work. The middle chapters (ep04 to ep07) ask for Fermi estimates and journaling with less scaffolding and no answers: theological reasoning, translation practice, mechanism hunts. The final chapters (ep08 to ep10) demand unaided first-principles reasoning: decision theory under the multiverse, attention economics, counterfactual audits done from scratch. Do every drill with your own brain before touching AI.

5. **[Ep 5 · Peter Thiel 2015](ep05.md)**: definite versus indefinite optimism: atoms against bits, the $100K to $1B gap, hysteresis, vertical progress, and substance over status.
6. **[Ep 6 · Jhumpa Lahiri](ep06.md)**: translation as belonging: the taped Bengali method, voluntary blindness as a lens, and why a book cover can feel like trauma.
7. **[Ep 7 · Steven Pinker](ep07.md)**: the two-machine theory of language, the Hayekian economics of irregular verbs, the $10,000 Hiroshima test, and the euphemism treadmill.

10. **[Ep 10 · Niall Ferguson](ep10.md)**: the counterfactual as production function: Collingwood's reconstitution, pricing the mundane middle, and the Empire cost-benefit audit.
