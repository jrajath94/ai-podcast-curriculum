## Why this episode matters

This is the track's scale episode: what it actually takes to build at the frontier. Zuckerberg gives you the numbers no one else gives: the path from millions to $10B training runs, what a 1GW datacenter requires, and why Meta open-sourced Llama when everyone else was closing. It is also the oldest episode in the track (April 2024), which makes it a time capsule: read it as a prediction from 2024 about 2026, then check it against the later episodes. And it contains the track's most unexpected segment: Caesar Augustus, and a theory of power that explains Meta's strategy better than any business analysis.

## The story

### Why open-source Llama?

The strategic logic, as Zuckerberg presents it. Meta is not a model vendor; it is a product company that uses models. Open-sourcing Llama commoditizes the layer Meta does not monetize (the model) and strengthens the layers it does (the products, the ecosystem). Every developer building on Llama is building on Meta's stack, finding its bugs, extending its reach.

The deeper logic: open source creates the ecosystem that closed models cannot buy. Researchers, startups, and tinkerers improve the commons; Meta harvests the improvements. It is the Android strategy applied to AI: commoditize your complement.

### The path to $10B models

The episode traces the scaling of training runs: from millions to hundreds of millions to billions, and the trajectory toward $10B runs. The drivers:

- Compute: more GPUs, longer runs, bigger clusters.
- Data: more tokens, better quality, the scaling laws holding.
- The economics: each generation costs an order of magnitude more and produces a qualitatively better model, which justifies the next order of magnitude.

The $10B number is not a boast; it is a planning figure. If you believe the scaling laws, you plan the infrastructure for the next zero.

### 1 GW datacenters

A gigawatt datacenter is a power plant with computers in it. The episode covers what this requires:

- Power: 1GW of reliable electricity, which constrains where you can build (near generation, with grid capacity).
- Cooling: at gigawatt scale, cooling is a first-order engineering problem.
- Networking: the cluster must act as one machine (connecting to ep07's factory thesis).
- The supply chain: GPUs, power equipment, construction, all on timelines measured in years.

This is the physical reality behind the abstract "compute" of the other episodes. Compute is electricity, concrete, and cooling.

### Llama 3

The episode covers Llama 3's release: the model, the capabilities, the open weights. The technical details matter less than the strategic pattern: each Llama release raises the floor for the whole industry, which is exactly what a commoditize-your-complement strategy wants. The better the open models, the less pricing power the closed labs have, and the more value accrues to the products built on top.

## Questions and answers

**1. Why did Meta open-source Llama instead of selling it?**

Commoditize your complement. Meta monetizes products, not models. Open weights build the ecosystem (developers, researchers, startups) that improves the commons Meta harvests. Every Llama builder strengthens Meta's stack and weakens closed labs' pricing power. It is Android applied to AI.

**2. What does the path to $10B training runs look like?**

Each generation costs ~10x more: millions → hundreds of millions → billions → $10B. The drivers are compute (bigger clusters, longer runs), data (more tokens, scaling laws holding), and economics (each generation's quality jump justifies the next 10x). Plan infrastructure for the next zero.

**3. What does a 1GW datacenter actually require?**

A gigawatt of power (constraining location to generation + grid), gigawatt-scale cooling, cluster-as-one-machine networking, and a multi-year supply chain for GPUs, power equipment, and construction. Compute is electricity, concrete, and cooling.

**4. How does this connect to ep07 (Nvidia)?**

Ep07 explains why the GPUs cost what they cost (the full stack). Ep08 explains what you build with them (the gigawatt factory) and what the training runs cost ($10B). Supplier economics → factory economics → model economics. One chain.

**5. Applied: our company is deciding open vs closed for our AI work. What do we take from this?**

Ask what you monetize. If you monetize the model, closing protects revenue. If you monetize products built on models, opening commoditizes your costs and recruits the world to improve your inputs. Most companies are Meta-like (products), not lab-like (models). Act accordingly.

**6. What is the Augustus theory of power?**

Make power structural, not personal. Augustus built institutions that outlasted him. The Meta parallel: the open ecosystem (Llama, developers) as the institution, rather than any single model. Structures persist; moments do not.

**7. This episode is from April 2024. How do I read it?**

As a prediction. Check its claims against the 2025-2026 episodes: did training runs hit the projected scales? Did open models raise the floor as predicted? The time capsule is the feature: it lets you score a 2024 forecast with 2026 data.
