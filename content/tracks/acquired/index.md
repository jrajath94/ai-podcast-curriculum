## The track

Acquired is the podcast about great technology companies and the stories and playbooks behind them. This track takes its ten most-viewed AI-relevant episodes (ranked by verified YouTube views, October 2026) and rebuilds each as a textbook chapter: the full story, the numbers worked by hand, the strategy extracted, and drills that train you to think.

The spine of the track is the complete NVIDIA trilogy plus the Jensen Huang interview: the founding (1993–2006), the CUDA decade (2006–2022), and the AI detonation (2022–2023). Around it: TSMC, Meta, Microsoft, and Google: the foundry, the acquirer, the platform, and the AI company. This track also carries the curriculum's hardware core: why NVIDIA is expensive, what is inside a GPU and a rack, and how to build an AI cluster.

## The intelligence-thread arc

The drills in "Think it yourself" get progressively harder across the track, by design. Early chapters (ep01–ep03) give you guided computations with answers shown. The middle chapters (ep04–ep07) ask for Fermi estimates and journaling with less scaffolding. The final chapters (ep08–ep11) demand unaided reasoning: memos written without hindsight, cluster designs with all arithmetic shown, attack plans graded for honesty. The curriculum trains you to THINK, not just informs you. Do every drill with your own brain before touching AI.

## Episodes

Each line is why the episode matters: what it gives you that no other episode does.

1. **[Ep 1 · Jensen Huang](ep01.md)**: the only first-person account of betting a $20B company on a market that did not exist, and the management system (40+ direct reports, no 1:1s) that made it possible.
2. **[Ep 2 · TSMC](ep02.md)**: the foundry playbook: why the company that manufactures everyone else's chips is the most defensible business in semiconductors, told through the 2009 crisis.
3. **[Ep 3 · Zuckerberg](ep03.md)**: the learn-faster loop as a company operating system, and the clearest explanation anywhere of why a rational CEO open-sources.
4. **[Ep 4 · Google III](ep04.md)**: the innovator's dilemma applied to the company that invented the transformer and still almost lost AI, plus the only 7-powers moat analysis in the track.
5. **[Ep 5 · Google I](ep05.md)**: the original startup playbook: how a better algorithm plus a better auction beat incumbents who had every advantage.
6. **[Ep 6 · Microsoft](ep06.md)**: the art of the hedge: licensing QDOS for $75K, partnering with IBM while building its escape hatch, and keeping 49% of the company.
7. **[Ep 7 · Meta](ep07.md)**: how acquisitions buy time (the 2012 email), how growth became a science, and the coldest math in the track: the $60B Reality Labs hedge.
8. **[Ep 8 · Nvidia II](ep08.md)**: the patience story: an 80% stock collapse survived by continuing to build, and the decade-long lag between being right and being believed.
9. **[Ep 9 · Nvidia I](ep09.md)**: the survival story: two near-deaths, the six-month cadence that killed 90 competitors, and why naming the category ("GPU") is free permanent positioning.
10. **[Ep 10 · Nvidia III](ep10.md)**: the moat, quantified: $40K per H100, 10,000 person-years of CUDA, booked TSMC capacity, the pricing anatomy of dominance.
11. **[Ep 11 · Hardware synthesis](ep11-synthesis.md)**: the only place in the curriculum that opens the machine: H100 internals, the DGX rack spec sheet, and a worked 1,000-GPU cluster build, with episode claims and added sources labeled.
